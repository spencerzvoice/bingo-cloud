# The funnels — A through M

**Revised:** 23 September 2026 · **Owner:** the `vo-lead-batch` workflow
**Convention:** every company in the pipeline carries exactly one funnel letter. The letter describes *what kind of client they are*, not when they were found.

A–E are the original structure Spencer defined. F–M were derived on 23 Sep from the ~132 companies the daily batches had actually turned up, which no longer fit inside five letters. The rule for adding a letter: a group earns one when its **buying pattern and pitch angle** differ, not merely its industry label.

---

## A — Design-led SaaS

Design and creative tools, website builders.

- **What they make:** product-launch films, brand manifestos, feature explainers 30–90s, social cutdowns.
- **Cadence:** launches roughly quarterly; explainers monthly to biweekly.
- **Economics:** lower volume, higher rate, commercial-adjacent budgets. They care how it sounds.
- **Retainer shape:** *"the voice of every feature video plus the launch films."*
- **Door:** Executive Producer, Head of Brand Studio, Brand Producer.

## B — Work & productivity SaaS with an in-house video team

Project management, comms, docs, CRM, marketing automation.

- **What they make:** product explainers and demos (the bulk), tutorial series, YouTube operations, onboarding, webinar and event sizzles, customer stories.
- **Cadence:** weekly, often several a week.
- **Economics:** the **best retainer funnel.** Volume is the whole argument.
- **Retainer shape:** *"our in-house narrator"* — batched monthly, 24h turnaround, locked rate.
- **Door:** Video Production Manager, Senior Video Producer, Creative Producer.

## C — E-learning platforms

Consumer and prosumer course marketplaces and learning platforms.

- **Target their MARKETING output, not their course catalogue.** Courses are narrated by the instructors who teach them; that work is not for hire. Brand campaign films, product tours, learner story videos and enterprise sales video are.
- **Cadence:** campaign-driven, monthly at best.
- **Retainer shape:** *"preferred voice for the marketing team."*
- **One standing exception:** IxDF, where the platform produces its own course narration in-house — there the course work *is* the product and the volume is steadier than the marketing angle.
- **Door:** Post Production Manager first (post supervision owns the audio chain), then Creative Director / Head of Content.

## D — Corporate L&D and LMS software

B2B training-authoring tools and learning management systems.

- **What they make:** product demos and feature walkthroughs, thought-leadership video, webinar sizzles, case studies, explainers. Articulate additionally produces sample e-learning courses to demonstrate its authoring tools.
- **Cadence:** monthly-ish.
- **Door:** almost always **marketing**, not production. Content Marketing Manager, Creative Director, Head of Content. Searching production titles alone returns false negatives here — see the method note in `ROSTER.md`.

## E — Streaming, promo and entertainment

- **PARKED.** Promo and trailer VO is a separate performance discipline with its own casting route through agents, and it needs a dedicated promo demo that does not yet exist. Contacts are banked; nothing is pitched.

## F — Developer and infrastructure platforms

Dev tools, data, observability, cloud infrastructure, game engines.

- **What they make:** technical explainers, conference and launch keynotes, docs and tutorial video, DevRel YouTube.
- **Angle:** the read is precise and unhyped. They buy credibility over warmth, and they notice when a narrator can pronounce the terminology.
- **Cadence:** steady, tied to release cycles and conferences.

## G — Enterprise and business software

Large B2B platforms outside dev and infra — HR, CRM, analytics, commerce, workflow.

- **What they make:** brand films, customer stories, event sizzles, sales enablement, product marketing at volume.
- **Angle:** big brand studios with real budgets and an Executive Producer who books talent properly. Slower to start, larger when it lands.

## H — Cybersecurity vendors

- **What they make:** threat explainers, product demos, research-report films, RSA/Black Hat event video, training content.
- **Angle:** the most consistent single vertical found so far — nearly every vendor of scale runs an in-house video team with a Video Production Manager. Tone is authoritative and calm; urgency without alarm.
- **Cadence:** high, and campaign-driven around research publications.

## I — Fintech and payments

- **What they make:** brand campaigns, product explainers, trust and compliance video.
- **Angle:** consumer-facing fintech (Cash App, Chime) buys commercial-grade brand voice; infrastructure fintech (Jack Henry, Experian) buys explainer volume. Different pitches — check which side a company sits on before writing.

## J — Creative and production studios

Animation, design and post houses that hire VO on behalf of their own clients.

- **Angle:** completely different pitch. They are not the client, they are the **gatekeeper to many clients.** One relationship with an Executive Producer here recurs across every job they cast. Lead with reliability, turnaround and self-direction, not with brand fit.
- **Warning:** studio staff churn fast. A verified Apollo address at a studio is the least reliable in the pipeline — confirm against the company's own team page before sending.

## K — Corporate and industrial in-house

Non-tech global corporates running in-house video units.

- **What they make:** internal comms, safety and training, investor and sustainability film, recruitment.
- **Angle:** enormous steady volume, slow procurement, often a vendor-onboarding process. Worth the patience — these become multi-year relationships.

## L — Consumer brands and travel

- **What they make:** campaign films, social, brand documentary, product launch.
- **Angle:** closest to traditional commercial casting, and the most likely to route through an agency rather than hire direct. Highest rates in the pipeline when they land.

## M — Institutions, nonprofits and media

Universities, charities, podcast and publishing houses.

- **Angle:** mission-driven and budget-constrained, but fast to decide and loyal once they book. Podcast networks in particular buy recurring reads.

## How a letter gets assigned

At draft time, not at research time. A company that is researched and banked already carries its letter in `ROSTER.md`; the Google Drive `Client Outreach/Funnel <letter>/` folder tree is created only for companies actually drafted in a run, because the re-voicing routine keys off those folders.

Two letters that look similar and are not:

- **A vs B** — both are SaaS. A buys a *performance*; B buys *volume*. Canva sits in A despite its size because its video output is design-led brand work.
- **C vs D** — both are learning. C sells to learners, D sells to L&D departments. The door is different: production titles work at C, marketing titles work at D.

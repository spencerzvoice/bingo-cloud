# VO Direction Cheat Sheet — decode the spec, then act it
_Built 2026-09-20. Tags: **[E]** = Elaine Craig, said in the Sept 19 workshop (transcript, ✅ read). **[W]** = outside source I opened (✅, named in Sources). **[B]** = Bingo synthesis / my own suggestion (🟡 — a starting point, not gospel; test it and tell me what works)._

## 0. The one idea underneath everything
Direction words are **not acting words**. "Conversational" "can't really be acted… means something different to everybody" **[E]**. Translate every adjective into **what you're doing to one specific listener** **[W: Rachel Alena]**:
- warm → *reassure* them · confident → *guide* them to the answer · conversational → *share something helpful with one person* · authentic → real intention, not imitation.

Then ask the three casting filters before you hit send **[E]**: **Believable** (have you lived it / researched the brand?) · **Relatable** · **Creative & unique** (imagine what everyone will do on line 1 — then don't).

## 1. Sixty-second prep before recording (Elaine's worksheet **[E]**)
1. **Last line first** (skip disclaimers) — sets tone; does it tie to line 1?
2. **Set apart line 1** — take your time; it must show you get the concept. If it's not locked, **repeat line 1 until you have a point of view** (edit the repeats out) **[E]**.
3. **Reformat**: kill ellipses, periods→commas, group lines, add contractions, note repeated words (rhythm).
4. **Find the transition** (problem→solution; anthem = 3 parts). Tone/pace shifts subtly there.
5. **One-sentence paraphrase** of the point.
Plus: **research the brand on iSpot.tv** — what tone do their spots/VO use now? Get "inside the head of the brand" **[E]**. Plus: **who am I talking to, where, and why now?** (busy workplace → project over it; "in your head" → internal) **[E]**.

## 2. Decoder — buzzword → what it usually means → how to get there
| Word | What they usually mean | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| **Conversational** | Thoughtful, one-to-one, not announcer. Ambiguous: can be relaxed/understated OR energetic/friendly — decide from who you're talking to **[E][W]** | Read "conversational" as **thoughtful** and slow down **[E]**. Pick a real friend + a reason you're telling them **[W]**. Reformat copy (contractions, commas) **[E]**. Swap "yes"→"yeah" where it fits **[E]** |
| **Warm** | Reassuring, welcoming, less formal **[W]** | **Talk to a child / bedtime story, physically smile** — Elaine's fix for your Genentech read **[E]**. "Blanket just out of the dryer" **[E]**. Slow down; a little closer on the mic for luxury/intimate **[E-other]**. Lift pitch on a few warm words (Elaine gave this to another reader; try it) **[E-other]** |
| **Friendly / approachable** | Someone you'd want to talk to | Relatable first: sound like "just a regular guy" **[E]**; contract, loosen, small smile |
| **Comforting / empathetic / compassionate** | Care, not performance | Put on the **white coat**: expert who does this daily, then bring it home to someone you love **[E]**. Slow and explain, don't add emotion **[E]** |
| **Confident / knowledgeable / authoritative** | Trust, expertise **[W]** | Guide, don't push. Purposeful emphasis + measured pace + clear articulation **[W]**. **You already read confident — don't reach for it** **[E]**. Bookend confident at the top/last line; be warmer/whimsical in the middle **[E]** |
| **Natural / authentic / real** | Imperfect & spontaneous OR polished-not-salesy (ask which) **[W]** | Think **telling, not selling** **[W]**. Take it off the page, say it in your own words, go back to copy **[E]**. Specific *lived* memory (Elaine: working returns) **[E]**. Don't sound like "how commercials sound" (singsong) **[E]** |
| **Sincere / earnest** | Believing it | Commit to one clear attitude from line 1 — "vanilla so I won't offend" loses **[E]**. Add hmms/awkward beats if it's comedy-earnest **[E]** |
| **Casual / low-key / no-sell / throwaway** | Don't push, don't care if they buy | "You shouldn't care if this person buys or agrees" **[E]**. Do one where you're not thinking about it. Gen Z/tech: "throw it away, here it is" **[E]** |
| **Fun / upbeat / playful / personality** | Enjoying it | Let personality out; find *moments* (the jersey line), not all-fun-all-the-time **[E]**. Twinkle on the odds/list, then build **[E]** |
| **Storytelling** | Something unfolds | Unveil, don't announce ("you have a secret" = what am I revealing?) **[E]**. Imagine the picture and let it unfold — **Buick: baby sleeping in the corner** **[E]** |
| **Anthem** | What the brand stands for — **not** "big" **[E]** | Support the picture, don't oversell. Let words breathe; lean in without punching **[E]**. Paint words ("design", "technology"), don't hit them **[E]** |
| **Luxury / relaxed luxury** | Unhurried, sure | Don't rush; hold the last word of each sentence; brand + product names warm **[E]**. Winning reads were "surprisingly low-key" **[E]** |
| **Energetic** | Ambiguous: pace? pitch? intensity? shorter pauses? **[W]** | Diagnose the gap (pace vs. emotion vs. brightness) and fix that one thing; don't just go louder **[W][B]** |
| **Serious / weight** | Gravity | Drop pitch a touch, slower, "not trying to sound nice" **[E]** |
| **Deadpan / flat / bored** | Dry, understated | Your **alternate take**: not selling, "here's the deal" **[E]**. Take the smile out, near-sarcastic. It won't sound as flat as you fear **[E]** |
| **Cool / edgy / sarcastic** | Sharper, less eager **[W: Keene]** | Flatten slightly, less smile, more attitude; keep personality **[E]**. Check the brand's actual tone first **[E]** |
| **Not announcer-y** | The most common pitfall **[W: VoiceOne]** | Any "delivering a message at people" sound — go one-to-one, smaller, thoughtful |
| **"Slick"** | Going through the motions **[E]** | Act like you don't have it figured out; talk to your best friend **[E]** |

## 3. Multi-word direction ("confident, knowledgeable, comforting, empathetic storytelling")
Do **not** paint the whole read with all of them. **Find the moment for each** — confident at line 1, Cleveland Clinic at the end, warmer/whimsical in the middle **[E]**. Bookend, then vary. Vary pace and emotion **line by line** so it isn't one-note **[E]**.

## 4. By genre
- **Radio / comedy:** No dead air — fill gaps with emotion/sighs **[E]**. Hit spec time but vary tempo: set up slow, list fast, land the end slowly and strong **[E]**. Start a notch quieter so you have somewhere to go **[E]**. Read straight first; improv **only** if copy says improv/"make it your own"/off-the-cuff **[E]**. Change replaceable words ("hey/yes/yeah") so line 1 stands out **[E]**. Radio needs more personality/explanation than TV **[E]**.
- **TV / online:** Pictures carry the story — more intimate, support the image **[E]**. Cuts → "see it" (Oh, look at that) **[E]**. Food on screen → linger on descriptors, romance it **[E]**.
- **Healthcare:** White coat → slow → explain **[E]**. Have a POV in line 1 — they may not listen to the end **[E]**. **Legal/side-effects: same warm person, just faster — no robotic switch** **[E]**. Ask "what am I saying?" on the first line's actual words (scientists, care partners…) **[E]**. Cleveland/Mayo: lower, more serious, weight from slowing down **[E]**.
- **Anthem:** see decoder. Brand names said like "the best name in the world" (Schwab) **[E]**.
- **Casual / food / quick-service:** Youthful, not slick **[E]**. Build: quiet/intimate → discovery ("there's the clincher") → lighter shrug **[E]**. Not one-note; build the list up to the payoff **[E]**.
- **Character / AVO spots (Indeed-type):** Read the AVO's line into yours — know what leads to your cue **[E]**. Genuine need beats "acting frustrated" — emotion at the end ("I need Indeed") is how they book **[E]**. Anchor to a real situation you've lived **[E]**. No partner? Ask yourself off-script questions ("what's the issue? what would help?"), then return to copy **[E]**. Boss = coach/cheerleader, not angry **[E]**.
- **Tech (Apple/Google-style):** Sometimes the throwaway Gen-Z read **[E]**.
- **Games/lottery:** upbeat, fun, twinkle on the odds, then build **[E]**.

## 5. Trick toolkit (grab one, try it, listen back)
- Talk to a **child / bedtime story + smile** — warm **[E]**
- **Blanket from the dryer** — cozy warmth **[E]**
- **White coat** — expert authority that stays human **[E]**
- **Best friend / the friend you have the most fun with** — un-slick **[E]**
- **Breaking-up voicemail** — earnest, awkward, comic **[E]**
- **Show your city to a visitor** — ownership, "this is how we do it" **[E]**
- **Something you love that you do with friends** — passion/ownership **[E]**
- **"Just do one where you're bored"** — kills over-selling, resets baseline **[E]**
- **Repeat line 1 until you have a POV** **[E]**
- **Lead-in phrase** in your head before the copy: "Have you ever noticed…", "Real talk…", "So this happened…" **[W: Keene]**
- **Get specific / activate senses / show your wrinkles** **[W: Such A Voice]**
- Give the **exact opposite for a take** (perky↔bored, TED talk↔whisper) to find range **[E]**
- **Walk away after take 10–12**; your best is usually take 3–4 **[E]**

## 6. Take strategy
- Take 1: straight, clean, what's on the page **[E]**.
- **Take 2: always an alternate** — quieter, flatter/deadpan, or a different POV. Not "better", *different* **[E]**. "Quicker and let it go" after you've done the work is usually your real read **[E]**.
- Double down if you're on the shortlist: the booked pro "doubled down on take two" **[E]**.
- Test: alone in the booth with no feedback, would you know it was a good take? **[E]**

## 8. Spec discipline (booking-killers)
- **Say the words right** — product/city names; a 45s read on a 30s spot signals wrong tempo **[W: VoiceOne]** (Elaine: a couple seconds over is OK if the read is great; a deadly-slow start on 60s copy isn't **[E]**).
- **Slate/first sound matches the character** **[W: VoiceOne]**.
- **Edits invisible; leave breaths unless distracting; over-clean 30s = suspicious** **[E]**. **Ride levels** so loud/soft lines even out **[E]**. Cut dead space before tag lines **[E]**.
- Casting directors disagree on breaths/lead-ins — aim for natural **[E]**.
- Audio quality must pass as studio-grade **[W: VoiceOne]**.
- Use script formatting (italics, bold, caps, quotes) as performance clues **[W: VoiceOne]**; quotes = room for personality **[E]**.

## 9. When the direction is vague — 5 questions to answer for yourself
1. Who exactly am I talking to and what's our relationship? **[W: Voice Realm]**
2. What emotion is inside this conversation (excited, skeptical, comforting…)? **[W]**
3. TV/online (support picture, intimate) or radio/audio (personality, explain)? **[E]**
4. What is the brand doing now (iSpot)? **[E]**
5. What one thing does the listener get? (your step-5 sentence) **[E]**

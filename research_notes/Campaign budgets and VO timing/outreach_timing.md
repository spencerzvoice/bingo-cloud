# Outreach timing and trigger-based prospecting for a freelance VO artist

Research run 2026-10-05. Tags: ✅ = page opened and read this session · 🟡 = seen only in a search-result summary or second-hand roundup, original NOT opened · ❓ = not found.
Almost every cold-email "study" here is published by a vendor that sells cold-email software or lead-gen services (Belkins, Instantly, Woodpecker, Salesforge, Gong, ZoomInfo, 6sense, UserGems). They are marketing content built on their own customers' data, not peer-reviewed. Treat the numbers as directional.

## 1. Cold email benchmarks: day of week, time of day, month/season

### Takeaway
The data agrees on a weak mid-week edge (Tue–Thu) and on mornings in the recipient's local time zone. It also agrees that December, July–August and holiday weeks are bad. The day-of-week differences are tiny, roughly 0.04 percentage points in Belkins' data, which is near its own stated margin of error. Seasonality and holidays move results far more than the choice of weekday.

### Cited Findings
- ✅ **Belkins 2025 study** (vendor; a lead-gen agency). It covers **7,530,489 emails and 34,393 replies, Jan–Dec 2025**. Reply rate = unique replies ÷ emails sent, excluding auto-replies and bounces. Belkins treats differences under ±0.05 pp as within margin of error. — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
  - Day of week: **Wednesday and Thursday 0.48% each** were best, **Friday 0.44%**. Monday had high volume but sat mid-table. Sunday showed 0.57%, but on only 21,615 emails, which is statistically unreliable. — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
  - Time of day: **8am–12pm = 0.54%** (best), **5–8am = 0.52%**, late evening = 0.40% (worst). Belkins notes this reverses earlier years, when evenings did best. — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
  - Month: **February peaked at 0.54%** and **December bottomed at 0.35%**. H1 2025 averaged 0.50% and H2 averaged 0.40%, a 20% decline. — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
- ✅ **Salesforge** re-presents the Belkins data with extra columns (vendor; secondary). It gives 12pm–5pm as 0.47% reply with 0.1% meeting rate. Founders/owners reply at 0.57% vs VPs/directors at 0.32%. Q1 averages 0.50% and is labelled "new budgets active". It advises avoiding December after mid-month. — [Salesforge](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/best-time-send-cold-emails)
  - The founder-vs-VP split matters for Spencer: senior in-house titles reply less.
- ✅ **Instantly Cold Email Benchmark Report 2026** (vendor; sells sending software). It covers Jan 1–Dec 18 2025 and claims "billions" of interactions across 700k+ users, with no sample size or reply definition published. **Wednesday is the peak reply day.** Monday is the best day to *launch* sequences, and Friday brings an auto-reply/OOO surge. **The report gives no percentages by day, no time-of-day data and no monthly data.** — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)
- 🟡 Growleads (agency, 400+ of its own campaigns): Tuesday is +45% vs the weekly baseline, Monday −18% and Friday −35%. Original page not opened. — [Growleads](https://growleads.io/blog/best-cold-email-timing-to-get-high-responses/)
- 🟡 A Gong Labs claim, as relayed by a search summary: afternoon sends (sender's time zone) out-reply mornings. **This conflicts with Belkins' 8am–12pm finding.** The Gong cold-email-stats page I opened has *no* time/day/holiday data and no sample size (published 2021, modified 2026). — [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-email-stats)
- Conflict on headline reply rates: Belkins gives ~0.4–0.5% per email sent, while Instantly gives a 3.43% average campaign reply rate. The definitions differ (per-email vs per-campaign/prospect), so the absolute numbers can't be compared across studies. — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates); [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)

### Inferences
- For Spencer's US/UK/EU targets, the defensible rule is: **send Tue–Thu, 8–11am in the recipient's local time zone, and avoid Friday afternoon.** He already schedules sends at 8am contact-local, which matches the data. Don't over-engineer weekday choice; the measured gap is within noise.
- February and Q1 are the strongest months in the one dataset that breaks out months. October–early November is the next-best window (see section 5).

### Gaps
- ❓ No creative-services / freelance-vendor-specific timing dataset exists. All data is SaaS/B2B sales outbound.
- ❓ Lemlist, HubSpot, Mailchimp, Yesware, Outreach.io and Apollo were not opened this session. Apollo's reply-rate page appeared in search but wasn't read. Mailchimp/HubSpot data is about marketing newsletters (opens), not cold-reply behaviour.
- ❓ No Europe-only day/time breakdown found.

## 2. Trigger-event / signal-based outreach vs untimed outreach

### Takeaway
The claim that trigger-timed outreach (new role, funding, launch, hiring) beats untimed outreach is near-universal in sales content. Every number found, though, comes from vendors that sell signal/trigger data, and most are unsourced. The direction is credible; the magnitudes (e.g. "18% vs 3.4%") should not be quoted as fact.

### Cited Findings
- ✅ FirstSales.io gives job-change emails an **18% reply rate vs 3.4% generic**. Its stacked signals reach "25–40%", and reply rates "drop measurably after 30 days", with the signal "substantially decayed" by 60 days. **The page cites no source for any of these numbers.** — [FirstSales](https://firstsales.io/blog/job-change-trigger-email/)
- ✅ The same page attributes to **UserGems** the claim that new executives evaluate new vendors **3–5x more often** than incumbents. It also attributes to UserGems that champion involvement raises close probability by 114%. These are secondhand attributions; UserGems' original was not opened. — [FirstSales](https://firstsales.io/blog/job-change-trigger-email/)
- 🟡 UserGems is widely cited for "new executives spend **70% of their budget in their first 100 days**" and "directors/VPs/C-level are **2.5x more receptive** in their first 3 months". It is also cited for "new-hire engagement +45% conversion, promotions +39%". Seen only in search summaries; vendor internal data; original not opened. — [UserGems](https://www.usergems.com/blog/pipeline-generation-buying-guide)
- 🟡 "Sendoso job-change campaign → 20% reply rate, 47 opportunities in a month". There is also a VP-of-Sales first-30-days campaign at 19.2% vs 3.1% control. Both come from search summaries of vendor blogs; originals not opened. — [Artisan](https://www.artisan.co/blog/trigger-based-marketing); [Autobound](https://www.autobound.ai/blog/signal-based-selling-complete-guide)
- ✅ Instantly lists "hiring patterns, funding events, product launches and website visits" as intent signals, but **publishes no quantified uplift** for them. — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)
- ✅ Woodpecker (vendor roundup, updated June 2026) gives **advanced personalization ~17–18% reply vs ~7–9% basic**, about 2x. **Small campaigns (<50 contacts) average 5.8% vs 2.1% for 1,000+.** This is a roundup that cross-references other vendors' reports, not clean first-party data. — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)

### Inferences
- The small-list and personalization data (5.8% at <50 contacts; ~2x for deep personalization) fits Spencer's model of 10-company funnels with a custom re-voiced sample. That model is structurally the high-personalization, low-volume end of every dataset.
- For a VO artist, the relevant triggers are a new Head of Video/Content/Brand (90-day window), a product launch or rebrand (new video assets needed), and a job post for a video producer/motion designer (a video pipeline is being built). A new agency-of-record appointment also counts (the roster is reopening). The evidence supports contacting within ~30 days of the trigger. 🟡 The decay figures are unsourced, but they are consistent across vendors.

### Gaps
- ❓ No independent (academic or analyst) study quantifying trigger uplift was found.
- ❓ No data specific to creative/freelance vendors (e.g. "new CMO → new VO talent") exists.

## 3. Budget cycles, shortlists and when vendors get added to rosters

### Takeaway
The best B2B buyer research (6sense) says most purchases are buyer-initiated, and the winner is usually a vendor the buyer already knew before the process started. That argues for being known *before* a need arises (top-of-mind cadence), not for perfectly timed pitches. Claims that "Q4 is roster/budget season" come from generic consultancy blogs, not data.

### Cited Findings
- ✅ **6sense** (vendor; intent-data platform). Survey of **934 B2B buyers, summer 2023**, all with purchases over $10k in the prior two years. Buyers initiated contact **83% of the time**. First contact came **~70% through the journey**. The average cycle was **11 months, ~8 of them independent research**. Buyers evaluated ~4 vendors on average. Contact before the 70% mark *reduced* win probability. — [6sense](https://6sense.com/blog/dont-call-us-well-call-you-what-research-says-about-when-b2b-buyers-reach-out-to-sellers/)
- 🟡 6sense 2024/2025 reports, via search summary only. The winning vendor is on the **Day-One shortlist ~95%** of the time, and 4 of 5 deals go to the "pre-contact favorite". First contact moved from 69% to 61% of the journey. Originals not opened. — [6sense 2025 report](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/)
- 🟡 Gartner: B2B buyers spend **only ~17% of total buying time meeting potential suppliers**, split across several vendors. The gartner.com page returned 403 and was not read; the figure comes from secondary sites. — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey); [Growth Method summary](https://growthmethod.com/gartner-b2b-buying-journey/)
- 🟡 Consultancy/agency blogs claim Q4 is when procurement reviews vendors and builds next year's budget, and that new line items should be pitched in budget season, "not in Q1 once the plan is set". Some say planning starts in August. These are opinion pieces from staffing/agency marketers, not opened, with no data. — [SimpleVMS](https://simplevms.com/turn-q4-planning-into-your-next-growth-opportunity/); [Conduit Digital](https://www.conduitdigital.us/insights/the-q4-playbook-budget-season-for-agencies); [ACG](https://www.aspirations-group.com/post/q4-budget-planning-starts-in-august-whether-you-re-ready-or-not)

### Inferences
- The 6sense findings come from $10k+ software/services purchases, a different scale from hiring a VO for a project. The principle still transfers: **producers book the voice already in their head.** Cold pitches mostly plant the seed, and bookings come later via buyer-initiated contact. This matches the VO-coach testimony in section 4 (clients reaching out months or years later).
- A VO artist is rarely a budgeted "vendor line item". VO usually sits inside a project budget, so annual budget-cycle timing matters less than project timing. 🟡 The exception is retainer/roster work with in-house content teams or agencies, where an Oct–Nov pitch to be "in the plan" for Q1 is plausible but unproven.

### Gaps
- ❓ No evidence found on when agencies or production companies add freelance talent to rosters. No casting-director or agency-producer interview on this surfaced.
- ❓ 6sense 2024/2025 originals and Gartner primary were not opened.

## 4. What VO marketing experts recommend (timing, cadence, top-of-mind)

### Takeaway
The VO coaches found all say the same thing. Follow-up is where bookings happen. The first follow-up goes about a week after initial contact, then every 2–4 weeks, then quarterly indefinitely. Holiday notes keep you top of mind. None cite data; this is practitioner opinion.

### Cited Findings
- ✅ **Paul Schmidt (VO Pro)**: wait **one week before the first follow-up**, then continue **quarterly**. He says it takes **6–12 touches** to engage a prospect. Many of his best clients replied only on the 2nd, 3rd or 6th+ email. Prospects regularly contact him months or years later, "multiple times quarterly", including people he hasn't marketed to in years. Systematized, this takes **<1 hour/week**. Targets: corporate video producers, creative directors, animation studios, ad agencies, casting managers, healthcare video and e-learning producers. — [VO Pro](https://vopro.pro/blog/the-voice-actors-survival-guide-to-direct-marketing)
- ✅ **Jillian Nielsen (Such A Voice, Jan 2018, older)**: follow up **one week after first contact, then every 2–4 weeks**, and **quarterly for demo recipients**. Tell the client when you'll contact them next, then honor it. She cites "seven exposures" before a response (no source). She warns that "Many voice-over actors stop following up after the initial contact when they don't hear back, yet that's the worst thing you can do." — [Such A Voice](https://suchavoice.com/2018/01/04/how-to-follow-up-voice-over-clients-agencies)
- ✅ **Marc Scott (VOpreneur)**, from a podcast episode page summary: direct marketing works, and execution is usually what breaks. He favours fewer, better-personalized messages, and treats follow-up as the differentiator because bookings often happen at follow-up, not first contact. No specific intervals stated on the page read. He also runs a "Follow Up Academy" and talks about a 30-day post-job CRM follow-up (🟡 from search snippet). — [VOpreneur](https://www.vopreneur.com/blog/332)
- 🟡 Search summaries of VO-adjacent content: Nov–Dec is the busiest period for commercial VO (holiday ads). January is often a planning lull at production companies. Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year are good times for a goodwill note or card. Pages not opened (Twine blog, Casting Networks; Casting Networks returned 403). — [Twine](https://www.twine.net/blog/voiceover-production-timelines-a-practical-guide-to-production-schedules/); [Casting Networks](https://www.castingnetworks.com/news/holiday-themed-voiceover-work-tapping-into-seasonal-narration-and-commercial-gigs/)
- 🟡 Casting Networks (search summary only): for winter/holiday work, pitch in October–November and follow up in December. — [Casting Networks winter guide](https://www.castingnetworks.com/news/the-actors-guide-to-landing-paid-work-during-the-winter-season/)
- ✅ Instantly (general B2B): the optimal sequence is **4–7 touches**. "Under four gives up too early and beyond seven diminishes returns". **58% of replies come from step 1 and 42% from follow-ups.** Step 2 should "feel like a reply, not a reminder". — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)
- ✅ Woodpecker roundup: one added follow-up raises total replies **+65.8%**. 3–5 follow-up steps give 8.3% reply vs 4.1% with none. — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)

### Inferences
- Spencer's current cold cadence is touch 2 at day 7–10, final touch at +14–21, then stop and revisit. That is *shorter* than both the VO-coach advice (quarterly forever) and the B2B data (4–7 touches). The evidence argues for converting "stop" into a **quarterly long-tail touch**, each with something new: a new credit, a new sample, or a reaction to their latest video. Going silent loses the people who would reply on touch 4–6.
- The coaches' "clients reach out years later" testimony lines up with the 6sense finding that buyers initiate 83% of contact. The cold pitch puts Spencer on the list, and the long-tail cadence keeps him there.

### Gaps
- ❓ **Not found this session:** specific timing advice from Dave Courvoisier, Paul Strikwerda (only a generic VoiceOverXtra "4-step formula" page surfaced, not opened), Gravy for the Brain, Debbie Grattan, or casting-director interviews. The VoiceOverXtra "Plan for NEXT Year" article URL now serves a "new website coming soon" placeholder.
- ❓ No VO-specific data (as opposed to opinion) on follow-up response rates.

## 5. Holidays, out-of-office periods, Monday/Friday effects

### Takeaway
Avoid Dec 15–Jan 1, US Thanksgiving week, the July 4 period (US) and August (Europe). September through early November is consistently named the cleanest window. Early January (around the 5th onward) is a low-competition re-entry point. Friday underperforms. Monday is fine for launching sequences but not the best day for replies.

### Cited Findings
- ✅ **ZoomInfo OOO analysis** (vendor; US-only, 2023 data, "millions of emails", **no percentages published**):
  - Worst periods: early April through late May (incl. Memorial Day), around July 4, and **Dec 15–Jan 1**. The single highest-OOO day was **Dec 27, 2023**. There is a Thanksgiving spike. OOO rates drop sharply after August and stay low through November.
  - **Best outreach period: September through early November.**
  - — [ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/out-of-office-reply-rates)
- ✅ Belkins: December is the worst month (0.35%) and February the best (0.54%). H2 2025 was 20% below H1. — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)
- ✅ Instantly: Friday shows an auto-reply/OOO surge. Monday is the best launch day, but Wednesday gets the most replies. — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)
- 🟡 Gong (via search summary): deal **sign rates fall ~38% in weeks with major holidays** (Christmas, Thanksgiving, Diwali, Hanukkah, Kwanzaa). This is about signed deals, not email replies. Original not opened. — [Gong (search result)](https://www.gong.io/blog/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says)
- 🟡 From search summaries of vendor blogs: the sharpest reply-rate drop comes in July–August across North America and Europe, and August should be avoided for European outreach. One unopened source claims EU reply rates run ~7–10% and US rates 15–20% lower, attributed to lower cold-email saturation in Europe. Unverified, and treat it with caution. — [Saleshandy](https://www.saleshandy.com/blog/cold-email-statistics/); [Outreaches](https://outreaches.ai/blog/cold-outreach-benchmarks)
- 🟡 January has the lowest overall mail volume, and the first week of January (around the 5th) is a clear window. This comes from a newsletter-marketing source (search summary), not cold-B2B data. — [io digital](https://www.iodigital.com/en/insights/blogs/the-professional-communication-dilemma-to-email-or-not-during-the-festive-season)

### Inferences
- For Spencer, as of 2026-10-05: **the next ~6 weeks (to ~Nov 20) is the best cold window of the year** by the ZoomInfo + Belkins evidence. Front-load funnel sends now and finish final touches before US Thanksgiving (Nov 26, 2026).
- Dec 14–Jan 4 is a send blackout for cold pitches. A short goodwill/holiday note to *warm* contacts (past clients, agencies) is the expert-endorsed exception. Restart cold sends from about Jan 5. February is the data's peak month.
- For UK/EU contacts, treat August as a blackout. Easter week also matters in the UK/EU (inference by analogy to ZoomInfo's US April spike; no EU data found).
- Spencer's personal commitment in the last week of November coincides with US Thanksgiving week, which is low-response anyway, so little is lost.

### Gaps
- ❓ No quantified Europe-specific OOO data (August, Easter, Christmas).
- ❓ ZoomInfo publishes no percentages, so the magnitude of the holiday dips is unknown beyond Belkins' monthly figures and the unopened Gong 38% sign-rate claim.

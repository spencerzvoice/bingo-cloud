## 2. Ongoing release rhythms and the human-vs-AI VO split

### Takeaway
Most companies now make video at least monthly (76%, Wistia 2026). Educational, product and social video lead the formats, and about a quarter of companies outsource to freelancers or agencies. AI is used mostly for scripting, captions and dubbing rather than full narration. One VO-marketplace survey summary says only 26% had tried AI voices, mostly on a small share of jobs. There is real pressure from AI but no wholesale replacement yet, and the data on it is thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Wistia 2026 State of Video** (900+ professionals; 13M+ videos and 79M hours of viewing data). OPENED — [Wistia stats page](https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/video-marketing-statistics):
  - 76% make at least one video a month. Fewer companies now produce daily, and more produce only "a few times a year".
  - Most produce in-house through video crews, creative teams or individual contributors. About 25% outsource to freelancers or agencies, and outsourcing rose 7% year on year. High-volume producers (250+ videos a year) are 41% more likely to keep production in-house.
  - More than a third use AI in their workflow and about 24% plan to start. AI is concentrated in pre-production (ChatGPT for scripting). The leading generative AI features are captions, then **dubbing** (Spanish, French, German, Japanese, Portuguese).
  - AI users make product videos at a 27% higher rate and ads at a 52% higher rate.
  - The most common formats are educational, social, product videos and webinars, each made regularly by over half of companies.
  - 2025 production budgets: about 40% under $5k and about 30% over $5k. Only 40% plan increases; 46% are flat.
- Wistia's Head of Production uses ElevenLabs to fix VO lines without calling talent back. Per Wistia, AI voiceovers "tend to underperform" human voices, though the gap narrows with a strong script and pacing. ## 3. E-learning/L&D production timelines, budget cycles and seasonality

### Takeaway
A finished hour of e-learning takes about 49–125+ development hours (Chapman Alliance, 2010 — old data). Audio sits late in the pipeline, after the script and storyboard are locked. L&D budgets are generally annual, planned in Q4 and often "use it or lose it", which points to two VO windows: a year-end spend-down push (Oct–Dec) and Q1 starts on the new budget. ATD's 2026 report shows learning hours up but spend per employee down sharply. That squeeze favours cheaper production, including AI voice.

### Cited Findings
- **Chapman Alliance (2010, ~250 organizations / ~4,000 learning professionals):** 49–125 hours per finished hour across e-learning levels. Basic e-learning (text, graphics, audio, quiz) averages about 79 hours, and Level 3 tops out around 716:1. Data is from 2010. SEARCH-SUMMARY (unverified); the original PDF was not opened — [Chapman Alliance PDF via CEdMA](https://www.cedma-europe.org/newsletter%20articles/misc/How%20long%20does%20it%20take%20to%20develop%20training%20by%20Brian%20Chapman%20(Sep%2010).pdf); [Christy Tucker](https://christytuckerlearning.com/time-estimates-for-e-learning-development/)
- **ATD 2026 State of the Industry (2025 data):** 16.7 formal learning hours per employee in 2025, up from 13.7 in 2024 and the first rise in five years. Direct learning spend averaged **$846 per employee, down $408 from 2024**. SEARCH-SUMMARY (unverified); the ATD page returned 429 — [ATD blog](https://www.td.org/content/atd-blog/highlights-from-atd-s-2026-state-of-the-industry-report)
- **ATD 2025 report (2024 data):** 13.7 hours, down from 17.4 in 2023. SEARCH-SUMMARY (unverified) — [ATD press release](https://www.td.org/content/press-release/atd-research-optimism-remains-strong-for-future-of-learning-in-organizations)
- Budget cycle: training budgets are allocated yearly and are often "use it or lose it", so unspent money doesn't roll over. Budget planning "often kicks off in Q4". Planning splits build (custom development) from buy (licensed content) and borrow. SEARCH-SUMMARY (unverified) from vendor blogs, not surveys — [Advised Skills](https://www.advisedskills.com/blog/corporate-solutions/use-it-or-lose-it-how-to-leverage-year-end-budgets-for-training-success); [Training Orchestra](https://trainingorchestra.com/training-budget-guide/); [Synthesia L&D budget guide](https://www.synthesia.io/post/ld-budget)
- Learning-industry survey figures (unverified): 89% of corporate learning leaders expect AI tools to be central within 3 years, and 72% of HR leaders expect a substantial AI impact on L&D. SEARCH-SUMMARY; the original study was not identified — [Learning Technologies exhibitor news](https://www.learningtechnologies.co.uk/exhibitor-news/future-learning-ai-voice-elevating-training-employee-development)

### Inferences
- In the standard ADDIE/storyboard flow, narration is recorded once the script and storyboard are approved, which is typically mid-to-late development. On an 80-hour build over several weeks, the VO booking comes late and on short notice. That rewards the VO a team already has on file over a cold pitch.
- At $846 per employee, down about 33%, custom-built courses with paid human VO are under pressure. Pitches to L&D should stress fast turnaround, clean audio, no re-record cost, and consistency across a series. Those are the arguments against TTS.
- For calendar-year companies, Oct–Dec spend-down and January kickoffs are the likely peaks. This is inferred from the budget-cycle sources, not measured.

## 4. How these teams source VO, and whether they keep repeat talent

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin. The Wistia 2026 survey gives the clearest number: about 25% outsource video production to freelancers or agencies, and that share is rising. Marketplaces (Voice123, Voices.com) present e-learning as a category where buyers value consistency and rehire reliable talent, but that claim comes from the platforms' own marketing. No survey was found that measures how often buyers rehire the same VO.

### Cited Findings
- About 25% outsource video to freelancers or agencies, up 7% year on year, and high-volume producers lean in-house. OPENED — [Wistia](https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/video-marketing-statistics)
- Voice123 says e-learning clients value consistency and "often rehire reliable, easy-to-work-with voice actors". It also says many repeat clients first find talent on the platform and the relationship grows from there. Voice123 runs an Enterprise tier for corporate buyers. SEARCH-SUMMARY (unverified; platform self-promotion) — [Voice123 The Booth](https://voice123.com/thebooth/how-to-get-voice-over-clients/); [Voice123 Enterprise](https://voice123.com/blog/the-voice123-platform/how-voice123-enterprise-works/); [Voice123 e-learning](https://voice123.com/pages/categories/e-learning-voice-actors/)
- Wistia's own production lead patches VO lines with ElevenLabs instead of recalling talent. That use case shrinks paid pickup sessions with the original VO. SEARCH-SUMMARY (unverified) — [Wistia webinar recap](https://wistia.com/blog/state-of-video-webinar-recap)

### Inferences
- Once a VO is the voice of a course series or product-tutorial library, switching costs are high: a new voice breaks consistency across modules. The first booking with an L&D or product-education team is therefore worth far more than its fee. This is inferred from the consistency argument and not measured.
- Pickups are where AI is starting to eat into repeat VO work, so contracts and quotes should cover pickup and consent terms.

### Gaps
- No data on the split between marketplace, agency, direct roster and in-house sourcing for SaaS or L&D buyers.
- No Fiverr or Upwork VO-category data was retrieved.

## 5. Practical signals a VO artist can watch

### Takeaway
No source in this session measured which signals predict VO hiring. The signals below are inferred from the cadence evidence above: event announcements, launch calendars, video-producer hiring, and casting listings tied to conferences.

### Cited Findings
- Flagship conferences publish dates 6–12 months ahead. Example: Figma's "Config 2026 is coming to San Francisco June 23–25" early-bird post came well before the event. SEARCH-SUMMARY (unverified) — [Figma on Threads](https://www.threads.com/@figma/post/DRAPh8zDDlO/video-config-is-coming-to-san-francisco-june-early-birds-your-time-is-now-to-grab)
- Conference talent is sometimes cast publicly (the Canva Create 2026 listing on Casting Networks). Page not opened (403) — [Casting Networks](https://www.castingnetworks.com/talent/project/canva-create-2026-live-conference-16024284/)
- Event rebrands create a need for new assets, e.g. HubSpot INBOUND renamed to UNBOUND for 2026. OPENED — [HubSpot](https://unbound.hubspot.com/blog/inbound-2026-returns-to-boston)

### Inferences
- Signals worth watching, in rough priority order (all inferred):
  1. An event save-the-date. Pitch 10–14 weeks before the event, ahead of the casting week.
  2. Job postings for video producer, motion designer or content/L&D designer. These mean a team is building capacity.
  3. A rebrand or an event rename.
  4. A funding round or a new product line.
  5. Q4, for L&D teams, given year-end budget spend-down.
  6. Casting Networks and Backstage listings tied to conferences.
- None of these were checked against data showing how often each one leads to a VO hire.

# Agency / Production Pipeline: Brief to Air, and Where VO Gets Cast

_Researched 2026-10-05. Source labels: **[opened]** = page fetched and read this session; **[snippet]** = seen only as a search-result snippet or a search tool's summary (🟡 unverified, the page itself was not read). Several key trade-press pages (Backstage, Campaign, LBBOnline, Ad Age, Casting Networks) returned HTTP 403 and could not be opened. That is noted where it matters. Much of the timeline material online comes from production-company marketing blogs, not trade bodies. Treat it as practitioner opinion, not standards._

## 1. Typical timeline from brief to air (TV/broadcast, digital/social, corporate)

### Takeaway
For a European/US broadcast TVC, roughly 12 weeks from concept to first air is the most specific phase-by-phase figure found. Post-production, where final VO is usually recorded, takes 2–6 weeks of that. Sources disagree a lot: some vendor blogs claim 1–3 weeks end to end for a simple spot. No trade-body (AICP/APA/ANA) timeline standard was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Overall: "~12 weeks"** from concept to first air for a commercial film production. [opened] — [Focusline (Swiss production co.), "Commercial Film Production Process: From Brief to Delivery"](https://focusline.ch/news/commercial-film-production-process/)
- Phase table from the same source [opened] — [Focusline](https://focusline.ch/news/commercial-film-production-process/):
  - Internal brief (brand side): **2–8 weeks** private sector; **4–12 weeks** public sector (procurement/legal/budget sign-off).
  - Agency selection (if a pitch is run): **6–16 weeks** competitive pitch; **12+ weeks** full review.
  - Creative development (agency strategy + creative): **3–10 weeks**.
  - Production-company sourcing ("triple bid", 2–3 competing companies): **2–4 weeks** standard; **5 working days** express.
  - Treatment & award: **5–10 working days** standard; **48–72 hours** urgent. "Treatment is the creative contract."
  - Pre-production: **2–6 weeks** standard; **6–12 weeks** international/complex. Ends with the internal Pre-PPM and the PPM (pre-production meeting, client sign-off). The casting director is listed among the key players here.
  - Shoot: **1–5 days** typical Swiss; **8–15 days** international.
  - Post-production: **2–6 weeks** standard; **6–12 weeks** VFX-heavy/multi-language. Offline cut → online conform → colour → sound mix; "voiceover recording, sound mix" sits inside post.
  - Delivery & trafficking: **3–10 working days** via traffic vendors (Adstream, Extreme Reach, Peach).
  - Talent buyouts "typically 12 months national all-media" (Swiss market).
  - Production fee "15 to 25% band on direct costs"; director's fee "7 to 10%" on jobs below ~CHF 680k.
- Contradicting view: a US vendor blog puts a *simple* TV spot at **1–3 weeks total** (pre-pro 1–7 days, shoot 1–2 days, post 1–7 days). It does not cover VO timing or approvals. [opened] — [Beverly Boy Productions, "Typical Timelines for a TV Commercial Production Schedule"](https://beverlyboy.com/tv-commercial-production/typical-timelines-for-a-tv-commercial-production-schedule/). Inference: this describes a small direct-to-client production company, not the agency + triple-bid model.
- Pre-production **2–6 weeks**; post **1–3 weeks** for standard TVCs, **4–6 weeks** for VFX/multi-version. [snippet] — [Cybertize Media, "How Long Does It Take To Make A TV Commercial? 2026 Guide"](https://cybertizemedia.com/blog/tvc-blog/tv-commercial-timeline/)
- VO-specific: "commercial voiceovers typically take 1–3 days from casting to final delivery"; urgent national spots can compress into one day, often with **50–100% rush fees**. [snippet] — [Twine, "Voiceover Production Timelines"](https://www.twine.net/blog/voiceover-production-timelines-a-practical-guide-to-production-schedules/)
- Corporate/brand video: production-company blogs describe the brief → creative → pre-pro → shoot → edit → finishing → delivery sequence and say approvals need explicit time. [snippet only, no specific week counts captured] — [Cardboard Spaceship, "Video Production Timeline"](https://cardboard-spaceship.com/blog/video-production-timeline/); [Decade Pictures](https://www.decadepictures.com/how-long-does-video-production-take/); [TriVision Studios](https://trivisionstudios.com/video-production-timeline-how-long-does-a-project-take/)

### Inferences
- The VO casting window is short and late. In a ~12-week TVC, VO casting-to-record usually sits inside the 2–6 week post window, and the casting itself takes days (1–3 days per Twine, snippet). The *decision about who gets asked to audition* happens before that, inside the agency producer's or casting director's existing contacts. A cold pitch has to land well before a specific job exists.
- 🟡 Digital/social campaigns: I found no sourced phase durations. Industry practice (unverified, my inference) is that they compress pre-pro and post into days or weeks and are often produced by in-house teams or content studios rather than triple-bid production companies. See the ANA in-house data in section 5.
- 🟡 Corporate/brand video: no sourced week counts were captured. Vendor blogs suggest weeks rather than months, with VO booked during editing.

### Gaps
- No AICP, APA, IPA or ANA document giving standard phase durations was found or opened.
- No sourced timeline split by digital/social versus broadcast. Ad Age, Campaign and LBB pages that might have one returned 403.
- No sourced data on how far before air the final VO is recorded (for example "X days before delivery").

## 2. Production-company bidding: triple bid, AICP bid form, award, post and audio post

### Takeaway
US agency production work is normally triple-bid: the agency producer sends boards to 2–3 production companies (and often 2–3 post companies). Each returns a director's treatment plus a budget on the standardised AICP Bid Form. Award goes on treatment, director and price, typically 1–2 weeks after the bid goes out. Audio post (where VO is recorded and mixed) is a separate vendor line, often awarded by the agency rather than the production company.

### Cited Findings
- AICP Bid Form: standardised commercial production budget format; introduced in the 1970s (sources say 1972 or 1975, which conflict) "to help standardize the bidding on commercials". It is the de facto standard. [snippet] — [Alaska Production Co., "What Is an AICP Bid?"](https://www.alaskaproductioncompany.com/resources/what-is-an-aicp-bid); [Frankfurt Kurnit (FKKS), "AICP Releases Updated Bid Form"](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102fvoe/aicp-releases-updated-bid-form)
- "Commercial production jobs generally go out to a triple bid pool of companies for both production and post." Firms submit creative treatments with a budget on a variant of the AICP form. The updated form now integrates production and post-production categories. [snippet] — [LBBOnline, "AICP Introduces Updated and Revised Bid Form"](https://lbbonline.com/news/aicp-introduces-updated-and-revised-bid-form) (page 403 when opened; date unknown); [FKKS](https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102fvoe/aicp-releases-updated-bid-form)
- AICP launched a tech platform for production bidding in Oct 2020. [snippet] — [MediaPost, 10/14/2020](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/356777/aicp-debuts-new-tech-platform-for-production-biddi.html)
- Triple-bid sourcing takes **2–4 weeks** standard (5 working days express); treatment-and-award takes **5–10 working days** (48–72 hours urgent). [opened] — [Focusline](https://focusline.ch/news/commercial-film-production-process/)
- Free the Bid: an initiative asking agencies to include at least one woman director in each triple bid. Evidence that the triple bid is the standard unit. [snippet] — [Wikipedia, "Free the Bid"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_the_Bid)
- Audio post houses offer an "agency model" where "a producer casts, directs, and delivers the finished mix." When an agency books a remote directed VO session, it runs through the studio's session-engineering pipeline (record, direct, edit, mix, deliver), and clients/directors join remotely. [snippet] — [Dallas Audio Post](https://dallasaudiopost.com/); [CMD NYC, "Voiceover Talent, Audio Post Production"](https://www.cmdnyc.com/audio-services)
- London audio/VO studios (Sonica, The Showreel, Soho Voices, Another Tongue) bundle casting with recording. Sonica runs "test reads of actual scripts for casting" and "audition days" compiled into clip reels for the client. [snippet] — [Sonica Studios](https://www.sonicastudios.co.uk/voice-artists/); [The Showreel](https://theshowreel.com/); [Backstage, "10 London Voiceover Agencies"](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/best-london-voiceover-agencies-69817/)

### Inferences
- 🟡 (inference, not sourced this session) The AICP bid form has a talent section, but on agency jobs VO talent is typically paid by the agency or advertiser as the SAG-AFTRA signatory employer, often through a payroll company, not inside the production company's bid. So the *production company* is often not the VO booker on agency TV work. The *agency producer* and the *audio post house* are.
- Audio post houses are a high-leverage pitch target. They record VO for many agencies, some run casting themselves, and their engineers and producers are asked "who do you know for this?" Several studios advertise in-house VO rosters (London examples above).

### Gaps
- The AICP bid form and guidelines on aicp.com were not opened, so the exact line items (including whether VO/talent sits in "Talent" or is excluded on agency-paid jobs) are unverified.
- No source captured on how agencies award audio post (separate triple bid, or a standing relationship).

## 3. When is VO cast, who chooses, through what channel; union vs non-union

### Takeaway
Two VO moments exist. **Scratch/temp VO** comes early (animatics, client presentations, offline edit) and is usually read by someone on the team, an editor or copywriter, or increasingly AI. **Final VO** is cast and recorded in post. On larger jobs a voice casting director or talent agents run the audition. The agency producer and creatives pick a shortlist (1–5 voices), and **the client makes the final call**. Casting directors do not book.

### Cited Findings
- Scratch tracks are placeholders "so the producer knows how much time they have to display certain visuals." [snippet] — [VoiceOverXtra / Tom Dheere, "If You're Asked To Voice A Scratch Track"](https://www.voiceoverxtra.com/article.htm?id=TSLC3HGH) (page opened but now shows "New Website Coming Soon!", so the article content was not available)
- "Once the script is close to locked, scratch voice over gets recorded to test timing and pacing… usually just someone on the team reading it (though these days AI voice tools work for this too)." [snippet] — [Max Make Motion](https://maxmakemotion.com/how-to-plan-a-motion-design-project-that-actually-runs-smooth/)
- Animatic editors sync "scratch dialogue — temporary recordings of the script read by actors, production staff, or professional scratch VO artists" to picture. [snippet] — [Saturation.io, "Animatic Editor"](https://saturation.io/film-crew-positions/animatic-editor)
- "Creative departments and production firms use someone from within the company to narrate a scratch track." Pro VOs charge "a micro-rate… especially if they have a shot at being your finished chosen voice" and "turn around a scratch track in an hour or two." No dollar figure is given. [opened] — [Kim Handysides, "Scratch Tracks What & Why" (2022)](https://kimhandysidesvoiceover.com/2022/07/12/scratch-tracks/)
- SAG-AFTRA rule: an audition cannot be used as a demo, audience-reaction commercial or scratch track unless the performer is paid at least the Commercials Contract minimum. VOs report around **$300** for scratch/demo/pitch tracks. [snippet] — [VoiceOverXtra / Tom Dheere](https://www.voiceoverxtra.com/article.htm?id=TSLC3HGH) (article itself not readable)
- Casting sequence (on-camera and VO): casting session → client reviews and names "selects" → optional callback → "Casting directors do not make booking decisions; the producers and director narrow down their selects from the callback and choose a first and second choice to present to their client, and then they all make the decision." [snippet] — [Casting Networks, "Parting the Clouds of Callbacks and Casting"](https://www.castingnetworks.com/news/parting-the-clouds-of-callbacks-and-casting/) (403 when opened)
- "Directors pick about 100 auditions to show to the client" (search-summary paraphrase; the source page is unclear, treat as weak). [snippet] — [Voices.com, "Voice Casting"](https://www.voices.com/blog/voice-casting/)
- Talent agents send breakdowns to their roster, "listen to all that are submitted, and choose the best ones to showcase to the client or casting director." Clients hire casting agencies/directors to audition and hire talent. [opened] — [Such A Voice, "Agencies"](https://www.suchavoice.com/information/casting/agencies/)
- Producers "invite all agencies for maximum options, then let agents self-select." DDO "keeps a highly specific roster to ensure that there is never any in-house competition." DPN is described as "the largest supplier of voiceover talent in the U.S." [snippet] — [Backstage, voiceover agencies articles](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/top-voiceover-talent-agencies-6256/)
- Shortlist sizes from named casting professionals [opened] — [Kim Handysides, "Voice Over Booking Tips From Agents and Casting Directors" (2021)](https://kimhandysidesvoiceover.com/2021/07/27/voice-over-booking-tips-from-top-agents-and-casting-directors/):
  - Liz Atherton (CastVoices.com, ex-TAG Talent): narrows to **3–5**; a non-viable audition gets "about 6 seconds".
  - Tania Giampetrone (Reisler Talent, Montreal): **2–3**; she invites people based on "sound equipment and previous auditions. If I know someone will do a good job and they broadly meet the specs then I will ask for an audition."
  - MaryLynn Wissner (Voices VoiceCasting, LA): **1–2**; **"65% match the spec"**, so **35% of bookings go off-spec**.
- 2025 SAG-AFTRA Commercials and Audio Commercials Contracts: 3-year deal with the Joint Policy Committee (advertisers + agencies), ratified 96.9%; wages +5%/+4%/+3%. A signatory (agency or advertiser) must be attached before a SAG-AFTRA member is used. Third-party signatories may serve advertisers directly only when no ad agency performs employer functions. [snippet] — [SAG-AFTRA, ratification announcement](https://www.sagaftra.org/sag-aftra-members-approve-2025-commercials-contracts-agreement); [SAG-AFTRA 2025 Commercials Contract MOA (PDF)](https://www.sagaftra.org/sites/default/files/2025-05/2025%20Commercials%20Contract%20MOA.pdf); [Davis+Gilbert, Letter of Adherence alert](https://dglaw.com/press-alert-details.cfm?id=1003)
- Effective **April 1, 2025**. Digital replicas need "clear and conspicuous written consent" at least 48 hours before use, paid at 1.5× scale session plus use fees. Synthetic performers are allowed, but "cost saving" cannot be the primary motivation and P&H contributions are due. P&H rate rose from 20.5% to 23.5%. The article does not address signatory agencies hiring non-union talent. [opened] — [Davis+Gilbert, "New SAG-AFTRA Commercials Contract Is Now in Effect"](https://www.dglaw.com/new-sag-aftra-commercials-contract-is-now-in-effect/)
- Voices.com markets its search to "people who work at ad agencies, audio post-production facilities, and video production houses." [snippet] — [Voices.com, "What is a talent agent"](https://www.voices.com/blog/what-is-talent-agent/)

### Inferences
- **Who decides, in order of influence (inference from the above):** client (final yes) > agency creative team (CD/copywriter, who often has a voice in mind from the scratch or a reference) > agency producer (runs the process, controls who gets invited) > voice casting director / talent agent (controls the audition pool) > audio post engineer/producer (suggests voices, especially on quick or smaller jobs).
- **Channel by tier:** big national US TV usually goes through a voice casting house or union talent agents. That is a closed door for a non-union freelancer on signatory jobs. Mid-tier, regional, digital, in-house and corporate work is where the agency producer, audio post house or in-house video team picks from demos they already know, online platforms, or direct contacts. That is the realistic door for a non-union VO.
- **Scratch is a real foot in the door.** If the creative team falls in love with the scratch read, that voice has an edge for the final (Kim Handysides says pros take micro-rate scratch work "especially if they have a shot"). A fast, cheap, good scratch read for an agency creative or editor is a legitimate wedge. Still, the job should be priced as a paid scratch, not free work.
- **Non-union caveat (🟡 inference):** big US agencies are SAG-AFTRA signatories, and their broadcast work defaults to union talent. The 2025 contract tightened who can act as signatory. Non-union VO lives mostly in non-signatory agencies, in-house agencies, production companies working direct-to-brand, corporate/e-learning and much of Europe.

### Gaps
- No primary agency-producer interview was opened describing exactly when final VO is cast relative to the offline edit (Backstage and Casting Networks pages returned 403).
- No sourced figure on how often scratch voices get the final booking.
- No sourced data on the share of agency VO cast via online marketplaces (Voices.com, Voice123) versus casting houses or agents.

## 4. Agency-of-record (AOR) reviews and pitch cycles; what happens to production after an account moves

### Takeaway
Account reviews happen year-round at large volume: more than 4,000 creative and media pitches in 2024, per search summary only. Marketing budgets for the next year often lock from September onward. No hard seasonality data was found. An account move resets the agency team, and with it the agency producer's vendor relationships. That is a plausible but unsourced opening for new vendors.

### Cited Findings
- "In 2024, more than 4,000 creative and media pitches shaped the agency landscape." COMvergence assessed 238 US media account moves/retentions in 2024 worth $12.9B (vs $13.1B in 2023). The 20 largest pitches were 59% of spend reviewed. [snippet; underlying source not opened] — search summary citing [Ad Age, "What separates agencies winning 2026 reviews"](https://adage.com/agencies/aa-2026-new-business-specialties/) (403) and related pages
- "Anxiety around agency structure and team continuity is driving an uptick in account reviews, particularly on the creative side." Mondelēz launched a creative review; IBM put media in play. Coca-Cola picked Publicis as US/Canada media AOR, replacing WPP. [snippet] — [Campaign, "How agency pitches are evolving amid industry shake-ups and the 'algorithm of record' race"](https://www.campaignlive.com/article/agency-pitches-evolving-amid-industry-shake-ups-algorithm-record-race/1944902) (403 when opened)
- The traditional AOR model is eroding in favour of project-based relationships. [snippet] — [Marketing Brew, Dec 5 2023](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/12/05/long-a-staple-of-advertising-the-days-of-the-traditional-agency-of-record-could-be-numbered)
- Competitive pitch **6–16 weeks**; full review **12+ weeks**. [opened] — [Focusline](https://focusline.ch/news/commercial-film-production-process/)
- "Some organizations lock next year's marketing spend as early as September, others wait until the first weeks of the new year." [snippet; agency-marketing blog, weak] — [Conduit Digital, "The Q4 Playbook"](https://www.conduitdigital.us/insights/the-q4-playbook-budget-season-for-agencies)
- Agency-review industry data publication (2025). [snippet, not opened] — [Agency Mania Solutions, Industry Update vol. 81 (PDF)](https://agencymania.com/wp-content/uploads/AMS_MIU_Volume81_2025_Final_Web.pdf)

### Inferences
- 🟡 When an account moves, the new agency's producers bring their own production, post and audio vendors and their own go-to voices. The *brand's* existing campaign voice may or may not carry over; brands with a signature voice often retain it. This makes "new agency just won account X" a timely hook for an audio post house or new producer team. It is not a documented vendor-opening statistic.
- 🟡 Budget-season pattern (inference): briefs written Q4 → creative Q1 → production Q1–Q2 for spring and summer flights. Holiday work is produced roughly August–October. VO for holiday campaigns is therefore likely cast late summer to early autumn.

### Gaps
- No primary data on seasonality of reviews (month-by-month pitch counts). Ad Age, Campaign and LBB were 403; R3 and COMvergence reports not opened.
- No source on whether production/VO vendors change after an account move.

## 5. How agencies and production companies choose and keep vendors and VO talent; how VO gets onto rosters

### Takeaway
Casting pros say they invite voices they already trust: known equipment, past auditions, "if I know someone will do a good job." Shortlists are tiny (1–5). So getting onto the radar is about repeated, reliable exposure to producers, casting directors and studios, not one-off submissions. In-house brand teams are a large and growing buyer of production. ANA (2018) found 78% of members had in-house agencies and 79% had in-house video production.

### Cited Findings
- Giampetrone (Reisler Talent): she picks who to invite on "sound equipment and previous auditions. If I know someone will do a good job and they broadly meet the specs then I will ask for an audition." Consistently shortlisted talent should "keep doing what they're doing… give something different at the end" (an alternate take). [opened] — [Kim Handysides, 2021](https://kimhandysidesvoiceover.com/2021/07/27/voice-over-booking-tips-from-top-agents-and-casting-directors/)
- Wissner (Voices VoiceCasting): bookings come from "talking to someone," and casting wants "your interpretation of that character," not "your best reading voice." [opened] — [same source](https://kimhandysidesvoiceover.com/2021/07/27/voice-over-booking-tips-from-top-agents-and-casting-directors/)
- ANA in-house report (**Oct 2018**, survey Aug 2018): **78%** of ANA members had in-house agencies (58% in 2013, 42% in 2008). **79%** had in-house video production, 49% of those built in the past five years. **70%** moved work from external agencies in-house over the past three years. **90%** saw increased workload. [opened] — [ANA, "Number Of In-House Agencies Rising Rapidly"](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/50728)
- A 2023 ANA survey reportedly found **82%** of brands have an in-house agency, and **22%** handle commercial production in-house. [snippet; 2023 report not opened] — search summary; see [Ad Age, "In-house agencies grow in popularity, ANA report finds"](https://adage.com/article/marketing-news-strategy/house-agencies-grow-popularity-ana-report-finds/2492486/)
- Most initial creative meetings involve "the producer offering a number of voiceover demos to help narrow the field." [snippet] — [Voices.com, "Behind the Scenes with a Video Producer on Selecting a Talent"](https://www.voices.com/blog/behind-the-scenes-with-a-video-producer-selecting-a-voice-over/)

### Inferences
- **Pitch map by stage (synthesis of the sections above):**
  - *Before any job (always-on):* agency producers / heads of broadcast production, audio post houses (engineers + studio producers), voice casting directors, in-house brand video/content teams. Goal: be in the "known, reliable" set they draw from.
  - *Creative development / animatic (weeks 3–10 of ~12):* agency creatives, editors and animatic editors need scratch VO. Offer fast paid scratch reads; the scratch voice can become the final.
  - *Post (last 2–6 weeks):* the final VO is cast in days. Only voices already in the producer's, casting director's or studio's pool get invited. Too late for a cold pitch to land on that job.
  - *After an account move or a new in-house team:* fresh vendor relationships are being formed. A timely moment to introduce yourself (🟡 inference).
- The in-house shift (ANA) means many brand-side video teams now buy VO directly, often without a casting director. That fits a non-union freelancer's direct-to-brand outreach.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on how often agencies or studios refresh VO rosters, or how talent formally gets onto agency "preferred" lists.
- The 2023/2024 ANA in-house reports were not opened. The 82% / 22% figures are snippet-only.

## 6. European specifics (UK, Germany, Netherlands, Portugal)

### Takeaway
In the UK and Germany, agencies and studios commonly book commercial VO through specialist voice agents and Soho-type studio-agencies, which run castings by sending sample reels or test reads. The APA (UK) sets the standard production contract with the IPA but has no published VO-booking guideline. Netherlands and Portugal: nothing sourced found.

### Cited Findings
- APA: UK trade body for commercial production, editing, VFX, audio post and music companies. It agrees "the standard form of contract for the production of TV commercials" with the IPA and sets recommended crew terms. No VO-booking guidance was found. [snippet] — [Wikipedia, "Advertising Producers Association"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising_Producers_Association); [Osborne Clarke, "New, approved commercial production agreement"](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/media-and-ip/new-approved-commercial-production-agreement/); [IPA/APA Interactive Framework (PDF, Jan 2014)](https://www.a-p-a.net/content/uploads/2014/01/IPA_APAi-Interactive-Framework-200114.pdf)
- London voice agents (The Joneses, Hobsons, Harvey Voices, Yakety Yak, Just Voices, Soho Voices, Sue Terry Voices, Qvoice, Voicecall, Loud and Clear) "cast voices for commercials… as well as booking studios and liaising with clients and artists." [snippet] — [Backstage, "10 London Voiceover Agencies"](https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/best-london-voiceover-agencies-69817/); [Sue Terry Voices](https://www.sueterryvoices.com/)
- London studios run casting: test reads of actual scripts and "audition days" compiled into reels (Sonica). Some hold in-house rosters (The Showreel). [snippet] — [Sonica Studios](https://www.sonicastudios.co.uk/voice-artists/); [The Showreel](https://theshowreel.com/)
- Germany: Sprecheragenturen (e.g. Stimmgerecht, 2,500+ speakers, Berlin studios; Sprechersprecher, 35 languages, "free casting") send several audio samples to the client by age and voice character, and the client selects. [snippet] — [Stimmgerecht](https://www.stimmgerecht.de/); [Sprechersprecher](https://www.sprechersprecher.de/); [synchronsprecher.de blog on radio-spot production](https://www.synchronsprecher.de/blog/radiowerbung-produktion-tonstudio-agentur-sprecher-radiospot-beratung/)
- Switzerland (as a European reference): triple bid is used, the PPM is the client sign-off gate, talent buyouts are typically 12 months national all-media, and multi-language VO is planned from creative stage. [opened] — [Focusline](https://focusline.ch/news/commercial-film-production-process/)

### Inferences
- 🟡 For an English-language non-union VO in Europe, the route into UK/European agency work is mostly via UK voice agents and studio-agencies (which hold English rosters) and via pan-European/international production for global brands, rather than local German, Dutch or Portuguese agencies, which cast native-language voices.

### Gaps
- No sourced information on Netherlands or Portugal agency/production VO practice.
- APA website (a-p-a.net) guidance pages were not opened. No APA document on VO booking was found.
- No sourced data on Equity (UK) versus non-union commercial VO practice.

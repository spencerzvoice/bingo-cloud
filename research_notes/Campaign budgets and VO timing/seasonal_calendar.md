# Advertising & VO Seasonal Calendar: tentpoles, production windows vs air dates, VO busy/slow months

_Research run 2026-10-05. Source labels: **[opened]** = page fetched and read this session; **[snippet, unverified]** = only seen as a search-engine result summary, page not opened (treat as 🟡). ~24 tool calls; several primary trade pages (Ad Age, Deadline, Casting Networks) returned 403/402 and could not be read._

## Q1. Production lead times for the key tentpoles (planned → produced/cast → airs)

### Takeaway
For the big TV tentpoles, the shoot happens 3–5 months before air, and the final VO is recorded in post-production, usually 1–3 months before air. To land in the window, a VO pitch to an agency or production company has to arrive **July–October for UK/US Christmas**, **October–December for the Super Bowl**, and **April–June for back-to-school**. The air month is usually too late.

### Cited Findings

**Super Bowl (US)**
- NBC sold the last ad unit for Super Bowl LX (aired 8 Feb 2026) in **early September 2025**, which it called "earlier than ever". - Most advertisers aim to lock Big Game creative ideas by October. Exception: Jeep's 2020 "Groundhog Day" spot came together weeks before the game. — attributed to [Ad Age FAQ](https://adage.com/super-bowl/aa-how-to-create-a-winning-ad/) via search summary [snippet, unverified; Ad Age returned 403]

**Back-to-school (US)**

**US 2026 midterm elections (3 Nov 2026)**
- AdImpact projects a record $11.6B in 2026 political ad spend (prior midterm record $8.9B in 2022). The split is broadcast $5.6B, CTV $2.6B, digital $1.6B, cable $1.4B and radio $273M. October is typically the busiest political month, and federal races crowd out broadcast and digital inventory. — [CNBC, 11 Jun 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/11/2026-elections-ad-spend-adimpact.html); [OAAA](https://oaaa.org/resources/2026-projected-for-historic-political-ad-spend-of-11-6-billion/); [Barrett Media, 28 Sep 2026](https://barrettmedia.com/2026/09/28/adimpact-radio-spending-2026-midterms/); [Campaigns & Elections](https://campaignsandelections.com/industry-news/political-advertising-could-weigh-on-media-market/) [snippet, unverified]

### Inferences (Bingo's reasoning, not sourced)
- **VO is recorded in post, not on the shoot.** For a UK Christmas spot shot in July or August, final VO falls roughly in **September–October**, with casting just before. For a Super Bowl spot shot October–November, VO falls in **December–January**. This is industry convention I did not source this session. 🟡
- **Ad inventory sells far ahead of creative.** Super Bowl spots sold out ~5 months early and World Cup spots more than a year early. Who is advertising is public by about September (Super Bowl) or the prior year (World Cup). That gives a list of buyers to target before they cast.
- **Midterm crowd-out (Oct 2026).** US brands facing pricier, scarcer broadcast inventory in October may shift to CTV/digital or move flights to after 3 Nov. That could push some Q4 retail recording later, into late October and November. 🟡 speculative.

### Gaps
- No primary source found or opened for production timelines of: New Year/January fitness and finance, Valentine's, Mother's/Father's Day, summer campaigns, tax season, or NFL/NBA/EuroLeague/European football season-start creative.
- No primary (trade-press) John Lewis interview giving the shoot month. The "heat wave" points to summer, but the month is not stated.
- Cannes Lions 2026 dates were not confirmed (historically mid-June). I found no evidence that Cannes affects VO booking.

## Q2. Evidence on VO industry busy and slow periods

### Takeaway
There is no hard monthly booking data from Voices.com, Voice123 or SAG-AFTRA in the public domain. What exists is anecdotal and conflicting, but it converges on three points:
- **Summer (June–August) is a slow period**, which Voice123 itself acknowledges.
- **Marketplace casting volume drops in November–December** even though holiday spots are airing. Those spots were recorded earlier.
- **January is a planning lull.**

Spring, plus August–September for back-to-school and corporate fiscal year-end, are named as busy.

### Cited Findings
- **Voice123 (Jan–Jun 2022 data):** "seasonality is a big part of VO and there is a summer lull". The period had 47,000+ roles completed, 100,000+ client searches per month and ~10,000 samples uploaded per month. No month-by-month breakdown was published. — [Voice123 "Voice Over Work Insights", 2022](https://voice123.com/thebooth/voice-over-work-insights-from-the-voice123-platform/) [opened] _(data is from 2022)_
- **The Voice Realm (casting site):** in November and December, "voice over casting sites experience a decrease in demand". Daily casting requests normally run 20–30 per day but drop to "5 to 10 requests on most days" in November–December, and the dry spell "can even extend until the end of February". Canadian VO Christina Ho is quoted: "I get very busy in the spring, and things do slow down heading into summer, but not drastically." The article says "there is really no singular pattern" because it depends on VO genre. — [The Voice Realm, orig. 23 Oct 2015, updated 26 Sep 2026](https://www.thevoicerealm.com/blog/voice-over-industry-slowdown-are-you-affected/) [opened] _(the core data is from 2015; the update date doesn't show the numbers were refreshed)_
- **VO Pro (Paul Schmidt, VO marketing coach):** "Just about everything in the voice over business slows down in June, July, and August", the "Summer Slowdown". — [VO Pro, 19 Jun 2024](https://vopro.pro/blog/what-to-do-when-its-slow-in-voice-over-yes-it-works) [opened]
- **Twine (freelance marketplace):**
  - Busy: November–December ("the busiest period for commercial voice work due to holiday advertising campaigns"), August–September (back-to-school and fiscal year-end corporate), tax season (financial services) and election years.
  - Slow: January ("post-holiday lull when many production companies are planning rather than producing"), July and holiday weeks.
  - Lead times: book 2–4 weeks ahead normally and 3–4 weeks in November–December.
  — [Twine blog, 10 Oct 2025](https://www.twine.net/blog/voiceover-production-timelines-a-practical-guide-to-production-schedules/) [opened; no data cited, opinion]
- **Conflict:** Twine calls November–December the busiest period for commercial VO, while The Voice Realm reports casting requests falling to about a third in the same months. One possible reconciliation: Twine may mean when holiday ads *run*, and The Voice Realm is measuring new casting *posted*. — see both sources above.
- Casting Networks has a piece on holiday-themed VO work, including advice to market same-day/12–24h turnaround in December. I could not open it (403), so I can't confirm its timing claims. — [Casting Networks](https://www.castingnetworks.com/news/holiday-themed-voiceover-work-tapping-into-seasonal-narration-and-commercial-gigs/) [snippet, unverified]

**Media-spend seasonality (context for Q4 "budget flush")**
- Q4 takes ~35% of annual ad budget (Q1 20%, Q2 25%, Q3 20%). November–December is the largest global ad-spend period. December is "spend it or lose it" on remaining annual budgets, and January is the slowest, lowest-CPM month. — [AdWave seasonal TV calendar](https://adwave.com/resources/seasonal-tv-advertising-calendar); [Freestar](https://freestar.com/why-january-is-a-chill-month-for-publisher-ad-revenue-seasonal-trends-explained/); [Playwire](https://www.playwire.com/blog/programmatic-advertising-seasonality-how-advertisers-spend) [snippet, unverified; ad-tech vendor blogs, not WARC/Nielsen/Kantar]

### Inferences (not sourced)
- The Q4 "budget flush" is about **media spend** in November and December. For production and VO, the matching flush likely shows up as **corporate/e-learning/explainer work in October–early December**, as unspent production budgets get used before fiscal year-end. That fits Twine's "fiscal year-end corporate" busy note, but it is not evidenced with data. 🟡
- For a freelancer, marketplace volume and the ad-production window differ:
  - Marketplace jobs (Voices.com-type) are short-lead and bunch up in spring and early autumn.
  - Agency/production-house tentpole work is cast 2–4 months before air.
  - Outreach to agencies should therefore peak **June–September** (Christmas, Black Friday, Super Bowl planning) and **February–April** (summer, World Cup or other sports, back-to-school).

## Q3. Regional differences (US vs UK vs continental Europe)

### Takeaway
Continental Europe effectively shuts down for the first three weeks of August, peaking around the 15th, so European producers are unreachable then. The UK works through summer, which is when it shoots Christmas. The US slows over the July 4 and Thanksgiving weeks. Late December is dead everywhere.

### Cited Findings
- August closures in Italy, France and Spain are strongest in the first three weeks of August, peaking around 15 August (Ferragosto/Assumption). Italy's "chiusura estiva" is a 2–4 week closure, and many French companies shut entirely for the month (five weeks' paid leave since the 1936 reforms). — [Whenoff guide](https://www.whenoff.com/guides/summer-holidays-europe-august); [Migaku 2026 guide](https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/italian-work-culture-the-august-shutdown-and-office-norms); [The American in Paris, Aug 2025](https://theamericaninparis.com/2025/08/08/why-everything-closes-in-france-in-august/) [snippet, unverified; general business sources, not ad-industry-specific]
- UK Christmas ads are shot in July and August (M&S from 12 Aug 2025, Sainsbury's in July, John Lewis "during a heat wave"). UK production is active in exactly the window when continental Europe is closed. — sources in Q1 [John Lewis opened; others snippet]

### Inferences (not sourced)
- **Lisbon-based, pitching Europe:** time continental-European pitches for late June–mid July or September. August emails to French, Italian or Spanish producers will likely sit until September.
- **UK:** July–September is prime time for UK agency and production-company outreach, while Christmas is in production and post.
- **US:** avoid the Thanksgiving week (26 Nov 2026) and 21 Dec–2 Jan for cold sends. These are common practice, not sourced.

### Gaps
- No ad-industry-specific source found on the August shutdown for agencies or production companies, or on UK/US Christmas-period closures.
- No data found on US Thanksgiving-week email response rates for B2B/creative outreach.

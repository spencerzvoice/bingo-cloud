# Win the shortlist before the brief lands

_Research synthesis, 2026-10-05. Built from five research notes in `research_notes/Campaign budgets and VO timing/`. **Tag key:** ✅ = the research run opened the primary page on 2026-10-05 (named inline). I did not reopen these pages while writing this report. 🟡 = search-summary, vendor blog or otherwise unverified. ❓ = not found. **Inference** paragraphs are Bingo's reasoning from the evidence. They are not sourced facts, and they are labelled so you can tell them apart._

**Bottom line:** **you will almost never time a cold pitch to a specific VO job.** Final VO is cast in days, late in post-production, from a shortlist of **1–5 voices** that producers and casting people already trust. B2B buyers also start the contact themselves most of the time (**83%** in 6sense's survey). So the goal is to be in a buyer's known pool before three moments: their budget is released, their tentpole goes into post, and their next launch is cast. Three findings set the calendar. First, **the next six weeks (to about Nov 20) are the best cold-email window of the year.** ZoomInfo's OOO data names September to early November as the best period, and this overlaps calendar-year companies writing their 2027 plans. Second, **Dec 14–Jan 4 is a cold-send blackout.** December is the worst reply month in Belkins' 7.5M-email dataset. Third, **February is the best reply month**, and it is also when the Jan-31 fiscal-year SaaS companies in your funnels (Snowflake, Braze, PagerDuty, Box, Skillsoft) get new money. US broadcast-agency work around the Super Bowl is mostly union and is already in production, so for you it is a closed door this cycle. The realistic agency window opens **Feb–Apr 2027** for summer and back-to-school work. The biggest change for your own system: your cold cadence currently stops after the third touch. **Replace that stop with a quarterly long-tail touch.** Both the VO coaches and the B2B data say a large share of bookings come from touch four onwards, and from buyers who come back months later.

## Final VO is cast in days, from voices producers already know

Here is how a broadcast spot gets made. The most specific phase-by-phase figure found is about **12 weeks from concept to first air**. **Post-production takes 2–6 weeks** of that, and "voiceover recording, sound mix" sits inside post ✅ ([Focusline](https://focusline.ch/news/commercial-film-production-process/)). The VO step itself is quick: commercial VO typically takes **1–3 days from casting to final delivery**, and rush jobs carry 50–100% fees 🟡 ([Twine](https://www.twine.net/blog/voiceover-production-timelines-a-practical-guide-to-production-schedules/)). Before that comes a **scratch VO** for animatics and offline edits. It is usually read by someone on the team or by an AI tool 🟡 ([Max Make Motion](https://maxmakemotion.com/how-to-plan-a-motion-design-project-that-actually-runs-smooth/)). Kim Handysides notes that pros take scratch work at a micro-rate "especially if they have a shot at being your finished chosen voice" ✅ ([Kim Handysides](https://kimhandysidesvoiceover.com/2022/07/12/scratch-tracks/)).

The people who pick voices describe a closed circle. In interviews collected by Kim Handysides ✅ ([Kim Handysides, 2021](https://kimhandysidesvoiceover.com/2021/07/27/voice-over-booking-tips-from-top-agents-and-casting-directors/)):

- Liz Atherton narrows auditions to **3–5**.
- Tania Giampetrone (Reisler Talent) narrows to **2–3**. She invites people based on "sound equipment and previous auditions. If I know someone will do a good job… I will ask for an audition."
- MaryLynn Wissner sends **1–2** to the client and says only **65%** of bookings match the spec.

Casting directors do not make the booking decision; the client does 🟡 ([Casting Networks](https://www.castingnetworks.com/news/parting-the-clouds-of-callbacks-and-casting/)).

The B2B buying research says the same thing at a larger scale. In 6sense's survey of 934 buyers, buyers initiated contact **83%** of the time, at around **70%** of the way through an **11-month** journey ✅ ([6sense](https://6sense.com/blog/dont-call-us-well-call-you-what-research-says-about-when-b2b-buyers-reach-out-to-sellers/)). The winner was on the **Day-One shortlist about 95%** of the time 🟡 ([6sense 2025](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/)).

The in-house shift moves even more of this buying away from casting houses:

- **82%** of ANA members run in-house agencies, and **61%** of work is done in-house ✅ ([ANA 2023](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/79185)).
- About **25%** of companies outsource video to freelancers or agencies, and outsourcing grew **7%** year on year ✅ ([Wistia 2026](https://wistia.com/blog/video-marketing-statistics)).

**Inference:** a cold email that lands during a project's post window is almost always too late for that job. The voice was already picked from the producer's pool, or by the in-house video lead, who has no casting director at all. Your re-voiced sample's job is to **put you in that pool before the next project**: it shows equipment, read and turnaround in one listen, which are exactly what Giampetrone screens for. This also means "timing" is two separate problems. One is **send timing**: when the email is most likely to be read and answered. The other is **pool timing**: being known before budgets and casting windows open. The calendar below handles both.

## Two "new money" dates: January for calendar-year companies, February for Jan-31 SaaS

Corporate budgeting is short. The AFP 2026 benchmark puts the average budgeting cycle at **8.7 weeks** 🟡 ([CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/cycle-time-to-complete-the-annual-budget-metric-of-the-month/655292/)). Practitioner blogs place calendar-year planning in **late Aug–Oct**, with approval 4–8 weeks before the fiscal year starts 🟡 ([CLIMB](https://climbtheladder.com/when-is-budget-season-the-annual-corporate-timeline/)). No primary, marketing-specific survey measures when marketing budgets are approved ❓.

The useful hard data is the fiscal-year table, pulled live from SEC EDGAR ✅ (`data.sec.gov` submissions API, field `fiscalYearEnd`):

- **Year ends Jan 31:** Salesforce, Workday, Zoom, Snowflake, CrowdStrike, Okta, Box, DocuSign, MongoDB, Asana, Braze, PagerDuty, Autodesk, Skillsoft, and Dell (~Jan 29).
- **Year ends Jun 30:** Microsoft (which includes LinkedIn Learning), Atlassian, P&G and Estée Lauder.
- **Year ends late July:** Cisco (and so Splunk 🟡), Intuit, Palo Alto and Zscaler.
- **Year ends late November:** Adobe.
- **Calendar year:** Coursera, Udemy, Duolingo, Chegg, 2U, HubSpot, ServiceNow, Dropbox, Datadog, Twilio, Figma and Sprout Social.
- **Private targets** (Zendesk, Smartsheet, Retool, Grafana, Zapier, Intercom, Descript, Pluralsight, Domestika): fiscal years unknown ❓.

The year-end "use it or lose it" flush is real, but smaller than it used to be:

- In 2017 it ran **from the week after Thanksgiving to Dec 31** ✅ ([Digiday 2017](https://digiday.com/marketing/irrational-budget-dumping-end-year-means-use-lose-marketing-projects/)).
- By 2024 it was "a lot less commonplace." Late money now goes to performance tests and is "not necessarily a net new partner" ✅ ([Digiday 2024](https://digiday.com/marketing/marketing-briefing-why-marketers-arent-focused-solely-on-use-it-or-lose-it-spending-in-q4-anymore/)).
- The first two weeks of January are slow 🟡 ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/some-publishers-report-that-q1-ad-revenue-is-pacing-10-25-behind-forecasts/)).

Budgets are tight overall, and tightest in B2B tech:

- Gartner put 2025 marketing budgets at **7.7% of revenue**. **IT/business services fell from 9% to 5.8%** while consumer products rose to 9.7%. **39% of CMOs planned to cut agency allocations** ✅ ([Chief Marketer](https://www.chiefmarketer.com/gartner-39-of-cmos-plan-to-reduce-labor-costs-and-cut-agency-allocations/)).
- 2026 is flat at **7.8%** ✅ ([Sword and the Script](https://www.swordandthescript.com/2026/06/gartner-cmo-trilemma/)).
- On video specifically: **almost 40% spent under $5,000** on production in 2025, and only **40%** plan to increase video spend in 2026 ✅ ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/blog/video-marketing-statistics)).

Procurement friction is small for small buys. Purchasing-card limits commonly run **$2,500–$5,000** before a purchase order is needed 🟡 ([Columbia P-card policy](https://universitypolicies.columbia.edu/content/purchasing-card-policy), a university proxy). Manual vendor onboarding often takes **2–6 weeks** 🟡 ([Raindrop](https://raindrop.com/how-to-fix-supplier-onboarding-from-weeks-of-email-to-days-of-automation/)). EU law sets B2B payment at a **60-day maximum** unless otherwise agreed. A proposed 30-day cap is blocked in the Council ✅ ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-a-europe-fit-for-the-digital-age/file-late-payments-directive-revision)).

**Inference:**

- **Your pipeline is split across two budget clocks.** Calendar-year edtech and SaaS get new money in **January**, and it starts flowing from mid-January. The Jan-31 enterprise SaaS companies get new money on **Feb 1**. They plan FY28 between **Oct and Dec** and are in their own fiscal Q4 over the holidays.
- That gives you a window calendar-year sellers don't have: **an early-January touch to Jan-31 companies, while they are still spending FY27.**
- The Digiday "not net-new" finding means flush money goes to **vendors already on file**. So any company you hope to catch in a December or January flush has to have heard from you **by about Nov 20**, to leave time for onboarding.
- The P-card evidence suggests **scoping first jobs** (one video, one usage term) so they fit under about $2.5–5k. Do this by narrowing scope, not by cutting the rate. Your rate discipline still holds. A smaller first scope just turns a cold contact into a vendor on file without involving procurement.
- Tight B2B tech budgets plus agency cuts favour a **direct, fast, single-freelancer** option over agency-routed production. That is your exact pitch.

Every tentpole follows the same pattern: the media is bought early, the shoot happens 3–5 months before air, and final VO is recorded in post.

- M&S filmed from **12 Aug 2025** 🟡 ([SWNS](https://discover.swns.com/2025/08/ms-turns-town-into-winter-wonderland-for-christmas-ad-in-middle-of-heatwave/)).

**Inference:**

- **Nothing that airs this Christmas or at the February 2027 Super Bowl is still open to a cold pitch.** Christmas VO finished in September–October. Super Bowl work is in production now, through mostly SAG-AFTRA-signatory agencies (🟡 inference: the 2025 Commercials Contract requires a signatory before union talent is used, ✅ [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/new-sag-aftra-commercials-contract-is-now-in-effect/)).
- The tentpole windows that matter in the next six months:
  - **Spring 2027 SaaS events:** VO booked about **5–9 weeks before** the event, so roughly Feb–May. Pitch **Jan–Mar**.
  - **Summer and back-to-school agency work:** briefs February–April.
  - **UK Christmas 2027:** pitch **June–September 2027**, outside this calendar but worth noting now.
- The ATD squeeze means L&D pitches have to win on what TTS can't match: **consistency across a course series, clean audio, no re-record cost, and fast pickups.** The Wistia note that its own production lead patches lines with ElevenLabs 🟡 ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/blog/state-of-video-webinar-recap)) shows pickups are where AI is eating in. Put pickup terms in your quotes.

## The inbox has its own seasons: September–November good, mid-December dead, February best

Send timing has better data than pool timing, though all of it comes from cold-email vendors.

**Belkins** (7.5M emails, 2025) ✅ ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates)):

- **February** had the highest reply rate (**0.54%**) and **December** the lowest (**0.35%**).
- **8am–12pm** in the recipient's time zone was the best send time.
- Wednesday and Thursday tied at 0.48%. Belkins treats differences under ±0.05 pp as noise.

**ZoomInfo** (US data, 2023) ✅ ([ZoomInfo](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/out-of-office-reply-rates)):

- **Best outreach period: September through early November.**
- Worst OOO periods: around July 4, early April to late May, and **Dec 15–Jan 1**.
- There is a Thanksgiving spike.

**Instantly and Woodpecker:**

- Wednesday is the top reply day and Friday brings an OOO surge. The best sequences run **4–7 touches**, and **42% of replies come from follow-ups** ✅ ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026)).
- Small campaigns under 50 contacts average **5.8%** reply versus 2.1% for 1,000+, and deep personalization roughly doubles reply rates ✅ ([Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/cold-email-statistics/)).

**Europe.** Continental Europe shuts down for the first three weeks of August 🟡 ([Whenoff](https://www.whenoff.com/guides/summer-holidays-europe-august)).

**VO coaches.** Paul Schmidt says to follow up after **one week**, then **quarterly**. He says it takes **6–12 touches**, and prospects contact him "months or years later" ✅ ([VO Pro](https://vopro.pro/blog/the-voice-actors-survival-guide-to-direct-marketing)). Jillian Nielsen says to follow up after one week, then every 2–4 weeks, then quarterly. She calls stopping after the first contact "the worst thing you can do" ✅ ([Such A Voice](https://suchavoice.com/2018/01/04/how-to-follow-up-voice-over-clients-agencies)).

**Trigger events.** Outreach timed to a new hire, a launch or a hiring push is said to perform far better. The magnitudes (for example "18% vs 3.4%") come from vendors with no cited source 🟡 ([FirstSales](https://firstsales.io/blog/job-change-trigger-email/)). Treat the direction as credible and the numbers as marketing.

**Inference:**

- Your current setup is already right on the small stuff: 8am contact-local sends, mid-week, 10-company funnels, and a custom sample. It sits at the high-personalization end of every dataset.
- The weak point is the **ending**. Touch 2 at day 7–10, a final touch at day 14–21, then "stop" is shorter than both the coaches (quarterly, indefinitely) and the B2B data (4–7 touches). Make touch 4 onward a **quarterly note that carries something new**: a fresh credit, a new re-voice, or a reaction to their latest video. Funnel A's "revisit Nov/Jan" rule (per MEMORY.md, not reopened) already points this way. Make it a system.
- 🟡 **Clock-change trap:** Lisbon moves to winter time on Oct 25 and the US on Nov 1 (general knowledge, not checked this session). For that one week the Lisbon–US offset is an hour shorter. Check that scheduled 8am contact-local sends still land at 8am.

## Conclusion

Timing does not decide a booking. It decides **whose shortlist you are already on when a booking happens.** The research changes what Spencer's outreach machine should optimise for. It should not try to hit a job's casting week; that is about 1–3 days long and invisible from outside. It should do two things. First, use the **two good send windows** (now to Nov 20, and Jan 5 to late March, peaking in February) to plant as many high-quality re-voices as possible. Second, turn every cold contact into a **quarterly long-tail relationship**, because the pattern in this research is that buyers come back on their own clock, months later, to a name they already know. The underused angle is the **fiscal-year split**. Most of the enterprise SaaS in the funnels closes its year on Jan 31, so early January is a live spending window that calendar-year sellers ignore. February then gives the strongest reply month and fresh budgets at the same time.

Two risks are worth carrying forward. B2B tech marketing budgets fell to 5.8% of revenue in 2025, and AI is moving into dubbing and pickups. Both favour a fast, consistent, direct human voice over agency-routed production, but only if the pitch says so explicitly. And the agency lane in this calendar is mostly about building the pool. The near-term revenue lane is in-house SaaS and L&D, which buy without casting directors, often under a card limit, and keep a voice once a series is built on it.

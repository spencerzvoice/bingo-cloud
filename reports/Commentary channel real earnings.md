# Rev-share only beats $100 past 165K views

Only three channels in or next to this lane have ever put real AdSense numbers on the record, and none has disclosed a sponsor fee: Internet Anarchist said one 1.5M-view video earned about **$8,000** (April 2023, roughly $5.30 per 1,000 views), Spill Sesh told the New York Times the channel averaged **$20,000 a month** from ads with $50,000 peaks (Nov 2023), and MoistCr1TiKaL showed a dashboard reading **$35.87M lifetime**, 97% from watch-page ads (Oct 2025). Patrick Cc has said only that sponsors are **10-20% of his income** and that he could not live on ad revenue until about 500K subscribers; SunnyV2, Turkey Tom and Moon have disclosed nothing, and every per-video dollar figure attached to them online is a tracker model. The best measured data for a new channel (AIR Media-Tech, YouTube Studio exports) puts a newly monetized Entertainment channel at a **median $153 a month at $2.81 per 1,000 views**, with 24% of new channels not reaching a single $100 payout within six months and only **1.5% of sub-1,000 channels crossing 1,000 subscribers in a year**. For Spencer the arithmetic is blunt: 25% of revenue equals $100 only when an episode generates **$400**, which on AdSense alone at the Entertainment median RPM is about **165,000 views per episode** (80,000 at the top-quartile RPM, 455,000 at the bottom quartile), three times what Hip Hop Madness itself averages today; the only way under that is a paid sponsor on every episode, which in this lane starts at roughly 40-50K consistent views. A cold-start channel pays the rev-share narrator close to nothing for 6-12 months; a spin-off from Hip Hop Madness could be monetized within weeks but, at the parent's 54K-view level, 25% of AdSense is about $33 a script. The flat $100 is the better expected value unless the deal carries a floor, and the sponsor line, not AdSense, is what would make 25% work.

## Three real AdSense disclosures exist, and none from SunnyV2, Turkey Tom or Moon

The disclosed numbers are few and come from interviews and on-stream dashboards rather than "how much I made" videos. Internet Anarchist (faceless, 1.87M subs, 287 videos, ~1.1 uploads a week since July 2021 per its About page) tweeted in April 2023 that "Sneako's Horrible Response To Penguinz0", at over 1.5 million views, had made "around $8K", which he called "life-changing money", before a Thumb Media copyright claim on behalf of Adin Ross diverted the revenue; the channel had just over 200K subscribers at the time ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/news-youtuber-discloses-losing-8-000-revenue-adin-ross-allegedly-falsely-claimed-video)). That is the single cleanest per-video AdSense datum in the niche, and it implies about **$5.30 per 1,000 views** on a US/UK-skewed drama video **[disclosed, 2023]**. Spill Sesh, the anonymous-for-five-years celebrity-gossip channel, told the New York Times at her Nov 2023 face reveal that near-daily uploads brought in "an average of $20,000 a month through ads" on 1,300+ videos and 350M+ views, with top months around $50,000 ([TIME](https://time.com/6331367/spill-sesh-youtube-anonymous-face-reveal)) **[disclosed, 2023; no monthly view count, so no RPM]**. MoistCr1TiKaL, on-camera and far bigger than this lane, showed analytics in October 2025 reading $35,870,022.16 from May 2007 to Oct 14 2025, about 97% from watch-page ads, with sponsorships excluded entirely ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/us/streamers/news-how-much-money-moistcr1tikal-made-youtube-twitch-streamer-reveals-earnings); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/10/16/moist-cr1tikal-youtube-ad-revenue-35-million/)) **[disclosed, 2025]**; against the 12.2B views on the same dashboard that is roughly $2.90 per 1,000 lifetime, dragged down by early years and Shorts ([Spilled.gg](https://spilled.gg/moistcr1tikal-reveals-40m-lifetime-platform-earnings-turns-off-fan-donations)).

Patrick Cc gave proportions, not dollars. In a February 2023 Creator Handbook interview (1.21M subs at the time, 2.58M today) he said "I'm fortunate to have really solid AdSense money. Not a lot of creators have good AdSense," that he "wasn't able to actually make a living off of my ad revenue until I had, like, half a million subscribers," that sponsorship deals were "about 10 to 20 percent" of income, and that he hired his first editor at ~150K subs and now runs three editors plus script and thumbnail help ([Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/finding-focus-and-building-a-business-on-youtube-an-interview-with-patrick-cc/)) **[disclosed, qualitative]**. Coffeezilla, adjacent and on-camera, said in Dec 2022 the channel "supports itself just on ad revenue" plus Patreon, takes no sponsors, and that the smaller "C tier" videos "make up the majority of the ad revenue" ([Coffeezilla Extras transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/coffeezillaextras/how-to-support-coffeezilla)) **[disclosed, qualitative]**. The researchers found nothing from SunnyV2 (4.41M subs, 1.465B views, Australia), Turkey Tom (1.73M subs, "USE CODE TOM at gamersupps.gg" in the description) or Moon, which could not even be identified with confidence beyond a fandom-wiki snippet describing a British video-essayist born 2002 ([YouTube Fandom wiki](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Moon)).

Everything else in circulation is a model, and the models disagree with the disclosures by an order of magnitude in both directions. SponsorRadar estimates MoistCr1TiKaL's sponsorship value at "$1.1K-$2.8K per sponsored video" for a creator who disclosed $35.9M in ads ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/moistcr1tikalgamingofficial)); Net Worth Spot models The Right Opinion, a 1.36M-sub channel, at "$600 in ad revenue a month" ([Net Worth Spot](https://www.networthspot.com/the-right-opinion/net-worth/)); and SponsorRadar prices SunnyV2's AdSense at "$30K-$89K per video" using a "Finance & Business" RPM of $12-35 that has nothing to do with celebrity commentary, on a page that itself says "Estimates, not actuals" ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/sunnyv2)). The table below keeps the two classes apart.

Two structural facts matter more than any single number. First, the two faceless channels at the top of this lane run as small studios: Internet Anarchist admitted in Dec 2025 that it had "recently expanded and brought in a new writer" whose script was plagiarised, let the writer go, and handed 100% of that video's revenue to the original creator ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2026/01/05/the-shocking-downfall-of-the-internet-anarchist/)), and Patrick Cc says "I have to make money so I can pay my people" ([Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/finding-focus-and-building-a-business-on-youtube-an-interview-with-patrick-cc/)). Nobody in the lane has disclosed what a writer, editor or narrator is paid per video. Second, the disclosures that exist all lean on AdSense, not sponsors: Patrick Cc at 10-20% sponsorship, Coffeezilla at zero, Moist at 97% ads. The "sponsors are 5-10x AdSense" story is told by vendor blogs, not by the creators in this niche.

## Sponsors in this lane pay the lowest CPM band and buy views, not subscribers

No channel in the celebrity-downfall lane has published a per-integration fee, so sponsor income has to be reasoned from benchmarks whose quality is weaker than the AdSense data. The only sponsorship dataset with a stated sample is Creators Agency's 4,000+ deliverables from 2021 to July 2026, and it is finance-only: about 90% of finance mid-rolls fall between **$50 and $200 CPM**, median near $100, with a dedicated video at roughly 2x a mid-roll ([Creators Agency](https://creatorsagency.co/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rate-card-2026)) **[agency-stated, with sample]**. Every source that covers entertainment puts it at the bottom of the table and none publishes a method: SponsorRadar lists Entertainment at **$10-20 CPM** against Technology $30-60 and Finance $40-80 ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/insights/youtube-sponsorship-rates-what-brands-should-pay)); 1of10 gives Entertainment $8-15 ([1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-sponsorship-rates/)); MilX, drawing on AIR Media-Tech's 300-channel data, says sponsored reads pay "$8 to $30 per 1,000 views" against a median AdSense RPM of $2.30 ([MilX](https://milx.app/en/cases/youtube-sponsorship-rates-how-much-do-sponsors-really-pay)) **[all estimates, no method]**. The one creator-relayed figure, NordVPN at up to $15 per 1,000 views negotiable to ~$20, is a c. 2020-22 Reddit claim relayed second-hand ([whatsabyte](https://whatsabyte.com/how-much-does-nordvpn-pay-youtubers)) **[anecdote]**. Applied to a 50K-view episode, the entertainment band gives roughly **$400-1,000 per mid-roll**, which is also where SponsorRadar's model lands Hip Hop Madness ($433-973) ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/hiphopmadness)).

The sponsor roster is consistent across the tracked channels and tracks average views rather than subscribers. Patrick Cc at ~1.4M average views carries Aura (15 integrations), Zocdoc (13), Underdog Fantasy, Morgan & Morgan and SeatGeek; Internet Anarchist at ~797K average views carries Cheeky (29 under two spellings), Scentbird, Odoo, Fum and Aura; Hip Hop Madness at ~54K carries one deal each from Freecash, TesterUp and Coupert alongside three each from Aura, Proton VPN and Invideo ([SponsorRadar: Patrick Cc](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/patrickcc); [SponsorRadar: Internet Anarchist](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/internetanarchist); [SponsorRadar: HipHopMadness](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/hiphopmadness)) **[tracker-detected, deal counts inferred from video content, fees modelled]**. The performance tier is what actually buys 20-100K-view videos: CreatorDB shows Freecash running an always-on programme of ~900-1,000 creators with a **median sponsored post of ~39K views** ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/brands/freecash.com)), while Raycon placed exactly one deal in twelve months on channels under 10K subscribers, with 42% of its deals at 250K-1M subs ([SponsorRadar: Raycon](https://sponsorradar.com/brands/raycon/get-sponsored)) **[tracker-stated]**. Gospel Stats' H1 2025 count of 65,759 sponsored YouTube uploads, up 53.9% year on year, attributes the growth to a "middle class" of channels averaging 100K-500K views per video ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/10/22/gospel-stats-youtube-sponsorship-landscape-report/)). What the trackers cannot say is whether Freecash, TesterUp or Coupert pay flat, per install or hybrid; Freecash's own public figures are its user-referral programme ($2.50 per first install, $10 at first cashout), not its creator rates ([Freecash](https://freecash.com/academy/en/discover/partner/affiliates)).

Payment timing matters for anyone paid as a percentage. Creators quoted by Digiday in Feb 2024 say Net 30 "happens about 50% of the time, and net 60 is more common", that Net 60 or 90 plus another month of chasing is normal, and one missed a mortgage payment over three late brands; a Tipalti survey found 56% of creators had faced late payment ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/in-a-booming-influencer-economy-creators-seek-standardization-for-payment-terms)) **[disclosed + survey]**. Realistic upload-to-cash on a sponsor read is 45-90 days, and AdSense itself pays only after the $100 threshold, between the 21st and 26th of the following month ([vidIQ](https://www.vidiq.com/blog/post/get-monetized-youtube)). A rev-share narrator's first sponsor dollar, if the channel gets one, arrives a quarter after the read.

## A cold-start channel earns under $200 a month after monetization, if it monetizes at all

The Partner Program gate is 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, with a review that takes "typically about 1 month" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)) **[official]**. Self-reports from r/NewTubers cluster at 4-10 months to reach it for channels that get there, with a 30-45-minute essay format hitting the hours well before the subscribers ([r/NewTubers search excerpts](https://reddit.sudovanilla.org/r/newtubers)) **[anecdote, threads not opened]**. The base rate is the sobering number: vidIQ's September 2026 index study of 32.9M channels under 1,000 subscribers found **1.52% crossed 1,000 within 360 days**, rising to 8.65% for channels adding 12-51 videos and 30.8% for 52 or more, a bucket inflated by Shorts ([vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-uploads-1000-subscribers/)) **[measured dataset]**. Of all channels with at least one subscriber, 7.9% have 10K+ and **1.3% have 100K+** ([vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-subscriber-benchmarks-2026/)).

Once monetized, the money is small. AIR Media-Tech's export of 297 sub-10K channels (Jan 2024-Apr 2026, Studio data) shows a median **$30 in the partial first month, $88 in month two, falling to $32 by month six**; 24% never reached a $100 payout in six months, 71% earned less in month six than month two, and the explanation is that channels usually cross the threshold on one video that takes off and then revert ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/how-much-does-youtube-pay-after-monetization)) **[measured dataset]**. The Entertainment slice (42 channels) ran a median **$153 a month at $2.81 per 1,000 views on ~54,900 monthly views**, and US-audience channels $106 a month at $3.46; by size, 6-10K-sub channels made ~$135 a month on ~93K views ([AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/how-much-does-youtube-pay-after-monetization)). For established channels, AIR's separate 300-channel study (10K-50M subs, May 2025-May 2026, 3,595 channel-months) puts the Entertainment median RPM at **$2.43, interquartile $0.88-5.02**, with only 55% of views monetized and the top 10% of channels taking 58% of all ad revenue ([AIR niche ranking](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm); [AIR 300-channel study](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/how-much-does-youtube-really-pay-in-2026-real-rpm-data-from-300-channels)) **[measured dataset]**. Internet Anarchist's disclosed ~$5.30 sits right at that top quartile, which is the honest ceiling for planning. Vendor planning ranges for "celebrity drama & news" ($3-6) and "commentary & reaction drama" ($3-8) are consistent but unmeasured ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-drama-channel-rpm-cpm/)) **[estimate]**.

The niche carries a monetization risk the datasets do not price. YouTube's advertiser-friendly guidelines say the "controversial issues" rules "may apply even if the content is purely commentary", that "singling out an individual or group for abuse or harassment" and "malicious personal attacks, slander, and defamation" earn no ads, that child-abuse-as-main-topic is limited ads at best, while non-graphic coverage of adult scandals and crime documentaries can earn ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278)) **[official]**. Commentary channels including Turkey Tom had monetized videos flipped to "Limited" in a July 2020 wave ([Reclaim The Net](https://reclaimthenet.org/youtube-creators-another-wave-of-mass-demonetization)), and Internet Anarchist's $8,000 Content ID diversion shows a single claim can erase an episode's revenue for weeks ([Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/adin-ross-copyright-strikes-4770/)). Under a flat fee that risk sits with the owner; under 25% it sits with the narrator too.

The best-documented year-one trajectories are not linear. Wani, an on-camera East-Asian celebrity-scandal channel and the nearest analogue, got ~10 views on video one, broke out around upload ten at ~15K views, and reached ~190K subs at month nine on roughly 36 weekly 15-20-minute videos, without ever stating revenue ([TubeTalk](https://tubetalk.buzzsprout.com/271511/episodes/19216428-how-a-commentary-creator-hit-190k-subscribers-in-nine-months-with-simple-videos)) **[disclosed, podcast]**. Tim Danilov's faceless Art of War went from ~15K-view videos to a 25M-view seventh video, ~$6-7K a month at month three and ~$50K in month six, but on a 15-60-videos-a-month Shorts cadence with a paid animation team, which is no template for one weekly essay ([TubeTalk](https://www.buzzsprout.com/271511/episodes/18611419-how-he-turned-a-faceless-channel-into-a-full-time-business-in-1-year)) **[disclosed, podcast]**. Veritasium's first year was "800 bucks", the second $12,500 ([transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/real-reason-veritasium-sold-channel/)). The pattern across all of them is flat for seven to ten uploads, then one video reprices the channel, or nothing does.

A spin-off from Hip Hop Madness changes the timeline, not the ceiling. Documented second channels clear the subscriber gate in days: Daily Dose of Pets took 2 videos to 1M views and 87K subs within a week of launch off a ~20M-sub parent ([ebizfacts](https://ebizfacts.com/jason-gryniewicz-daily-dose-of-pets-1000s-month/)), and Jarvis Johnson pulled ~300K views and ~50K subs to a tech spin-off from one video while his main channel sat at ~50K subs, the closest size match to Hip Hop Madness ([Dig Spaghetti interview](https://rosetta.to/u/digspaghetti/why-jarvis-johnson-left-an-audience-of-50-000)) **[disclosed, interview]**. None of these cases disclosed revenue, none is in the commentary niche, and the parent here averages ~54K views per video on 431K subs ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/hiphopmadness)), so the realistic expectation is monetization inside the first quarter and an early view level at or below the parent's, not Internet Anarchist's.

## The 25% share needs $400 an episode, which is 165K views on ads alone

The break-even is a single line: 25% of episode revenue equals $100 when the episode generates **$400**. On AdSense alone the views required are 400,000 divided by the RPM, and the table uses only measured or disclosed RPMs. Views here mean the episode's lifetime monetized total, which for a downfall documentary accrues over months, so the cash arrives long after the read.

| RPM used | Source and quality | Views per episode to reach $400 |
|---|---|---|
| $0.88 | AIR Entertainment 25th percentile **[measured]** | ~455,000 |
| $2.30 | AIR all-channel median **[measured]** | ~174,000 |
| $2.43 | AIR Entertainment median, 10K-50M subs **[measured]** | ~165,000 |
| $2.81 | AIR Entertainment, sub-10K channels **[measured]** | ~142,000 |
| $3.46 | AIR sub-10K, US top audience **[measured]** | ~116,000 |
| $5.02 | AIR Entertainment 75th percentile **[measured]** | ~80,000 |
| $5.30 | Internet Anarchist, one video, 2023 **[disclosed]** | ~75,000 |

Hip Hop Madness's own average of ~54K views would, at the Entertainment median, produce about **$131 of AdSense per video, or $33 to a 25% narrator**, a third of the flat fee; at Internet Anarchist's RPM it is $286 per video, $72 to the narrator. For the share to clear $100 from ads, the new channel has to average three times the parent's views at median RPM, or 1.4 times at the best disclosed RPM in the niche, on a format where the parent is the only audience it starts with. The established channels in the lane are far above that line: Internet Anarchist's ~797K-1.4M average views would, at $5.30, mean $4,200-7,400 of AdSense per episode and **$1,000-1,850 to a 25% narrator before any sponsor**, which is the prize the rev-share is really a bet on, and the odds of reaching it are the 1.3%-of-channels-at-100K+ base rate improved by an unknown amount for a spin-off.

Sponsorship is the only lever that brings break-even into reach early. If every episode carries a mid-roll at the estimated entertainment band ($8-20 CPM, plus $2.43 of AdSense), total revenue per 1,000 views is $10.43-22.43 and the $400 line falls at **18,000-38,000 views per episode**; but the tracker data says those brands do not buy below ~40-50K consistent views, so the practical sponsor-assisted break-even is **~40-50K views with a paid read on every single episode**, which even Hip Hop Madness does not have (22 tracked deals across a catalogue far larger than that) ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/hiphopmadness)). At the parent's level with a sponsor on an episode, 25% of ($131 + $433-973) is $141-276, above the flat fee; on the unsponsored episodes it is $33. A channel at the parent's size with sponsors on half its episodes pays the narrator roughly **$87-155 per script on average**, bracketing the $100, and that is the optimistic steady state for year one, not the starting point.

The year-one timeline under each deal follows from the data above. On the flat fee, 52 weekly scripts pay **$5,200**, paid as delivered. On 25% for a cold-start channel, months one to three pay nothing because the channel is not in the Partner Program; the gate typically falls between months four and ten, review adds a month, and the $100 threshold plus the following-month cycle puts the first cash two to three months after that, so first payment lands somewhere between month six and month thirteen; after that, 25% of the AIR Entertainment median $153 a month is about **$38 a month, or $10 a script** at four uploads a month, with the modal trajectory declining into month six. On 25% for a Hip Hop Madness spin-off, monetization plausibly lands in weeks on the Jarvis Johnson precedent, so AdSense share starts in the first quarter at the parent's ~$33 a script, and the question becomes how quickly the owner moves Freecash, TesterUp, Aura and Proton VPN onto the new channel; the first sponsor dollar then arrives 45-90 days after the read. A year at the parent's level with sponsors on half the episodes nets the narrator roughly $4,500-8,000, about even with the flat fee but paid late and unguaranteed; a year where the channel breaks out to 150K average views with sponsors on half the episodes pays roughly $12,000-24,000 ($91 of AdSense share plus $150-375 of sponsor share per sponsored episode at the estimated band); a year where it never leaves the AIR median pays under $500.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the choice as a bet on sponsorship and breakout, not on AdSense. Every disclosed number from the top of this lane says ads are the base of the business, yet at any channel size short of a breakout the ad share of an episode is a fraction of $100; the sponsor line is what carries 25% over the flat fee, and sponsors in this niche arrive only once a channel is reliably at 40-50K views and pay out a quarter later. The parent channel's own 54K average is therefore the pivot: a spin-off that lands at or above it, with the owner's existing performance-sponsor relationships ported across, pays about what the flat fee pays, while a spin-off that lands below it pays a fraction, and only an Internet-Anarchist-scale outcome, a low-single-digit-percent event even with a head start, pays multiples.

What this implies is that neither pure option prices the risk correctly. The flat $100 leaves Spencer with none of the upside if the channel becomes the next Internet Anarchist, where 25% would be worth a thousand dollars an episode; the pure 25% leaves him working for roughly nothing through the only period in which his contribution is certain. The structure the data supports is a floor with a share: the $100 per script as a guaranteed minimum, with 25% of episode revenue paid on top of or instead of it once an episode clears a verifiable threshold (the $400 line, or a view count in the 150K range), plus YouTube Studio read access and sponsor-invoice visibility so the share can be audited, and a written definition of "revenue" that names AdSense net of YouTube's cut, sponsor fees gross, and what happens to claimed or limited-ads episodes. If the owner will not grant a floor, the disclosures from Patrick Cc and the AIR data say the first year of a 25% deal is worth, in expectation, well under the $5,200 the flat fee guarantees.
